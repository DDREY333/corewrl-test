# Coreworld IV — Playthrough 1 Feedback (player answers, after Session 5)
Used to write the "DESIGN NOTES" section of coreworld-prompt-v2.md.

## Prompt and narration
- Most bothersome failure: the timeline being broken, i.e. inconsistent worldbuilding (e.g. Mother's "forty years" vs. canon "Drop 1 was her fifth"). Second: crewmates felt like puppets.
- Reply length: fine.
- OOC interruptions (Prep confirmations, retcon notes): good.
- Special actions (Pass the Time, Prep Action, Check Attributes; player-designed): good.
- NPC pushback: good, but should lean higher, especially from strangers.
- The "elephant" test (Fenn never checked in on R-9): the narrator did surface it (Mother said it at the mess); the player missed it. Non-issue.

## Difficulty (psychological / actual)
- Drop 1 Grey Orchard: 7 / 2
- Drop 2 G-31: 4 / 1 ("mostly waiting")
- Drop 3 0-31: 7 / 3 ("somehow the mud was the most dangerous thing")
- Drop 4 R-9: 5 / 4 ("an actual person for once")
- Drop 5 0-40: ~5 / 6. High points: the post counting faster than expected (the handler didn't know), and the soldiers creaking and turning. There was an illusion they might not make it.
- Varga's death: earned and fair, but too convenient; it's a cliché for the just-recruited character to die instead of a main one.
- Preferred consequences: tangible to the player AND in-world. Lost or damaged gear: yes. Injuries only if they affect the mission. Crew mental state: wanted, but it barely happened. Debt and social consequences: background noise unless tied to something felt. Deaths are a separate narrative matter.
- Whether Kade should get badly hurt or killed at this stage: declined to answer (don't spoil).
- Systems:
  - Post blink: great.
  - Hum/note: confusing on purpose, not yet fully explored.
  - Economy: weak; only useless things to buy, and Caps stopped mattering.
  - Dot's tiered offers: good (player proposed them; narratively sensible).

## Worldbuilding
- Cultists: underused, but that's fine (not reached yet). Other crews and the ship itself: definitely underused.
- Cares most about: the crystal hum/note mystery, and the cold things and "the quiet."
- Cared little about: the drag trail, the vanishing figure, the hook mark, the stairwell voice. These were not fleshed out enough. Fine if they connect to something not yet discovered.
- Dot handler: quite good.
- Ship wishes: more to actually do (train, buy upgrades, bargain, maybe a secret black market, something hiding in the tunnels/yellow lines). Less of a free-roam hub.

## Playthrough
- Most memorable characters: the AI (dot) handler; then Pip and Ostrow (tied); then Fenn and Mott (tied); then maybe Lusk. The stairway "guy" was memorable but not really a character. Mother was forgettable: she rarely did anything. Pip: the young kid who could figure things out, with a spotlight on Drop 1. Ostrow: the veteran mentor who always upheld rules and advice.
- Best moments: (1) on 0-40, learning the pylons/post were counting faster than expected and even the handler didn't know, so disaster became unavoidable; (2) finding the cold body in the pod and learning more about the quiet. "It moved" was the most memorable line.
- Drop ranking, favorite to least: 0-40, R-9, Grey Orchard, 0-31, G-31. G-31 was slower, boring, and had no tangible mystery.
- Openings: keep explaining very little up front. Crew size is fine. Roles felt generic and placebo: Kade "just driller"; Kade out-scouted the scout; Ostrow did everything except mining; Mother's medic role was almost never relevant. Pacing: narrator to be more specific (see Pacing Knobs in v2).

# Coreworld IV: Monster Design Work (Reference Only)

From the moderation chat of 2026-10-11 to 2026-10-12. Written so later moderation chats can learn how the user wants monsters and threats designed.

**Reference only.** Nothing here is narrator prompt material.
- The creatures and scenarios below are worked examples. Don't paste them into the narrator prompt: examples in a prompt become defaults that repeat every run.
- The principles that came out of this work already live in the narrator prompt (now `coreworld (playthrough2)/coreworld-prompt-v6.md`, `=== BEASTS ===` and DESIGN NOTES; the work itself was done on v4) and in `coreworld-moderator-prompt.md` (sections 4 and 6).
- **Open question:** does the user's own species (the Mimic, the Cyno) appear in play, or are they training examples only? It hasn't been decided.

---

## 1. How this work went

- The user's aim was to **train the moderator to invent thoughtful, original monsters on its own**, not to collect a bestiary. They said: "I'm training you to be able to make your own thoughtful and interesting monsters and entities. That's the main point of this."
- The method:
  1. The user gave their own designs one at a time.
  2. The moderator reviewed each with the self-critique method (weak spots, how each path plays out).
  3. The user answered the open questions.
  4. The moderator then designed its own, and the user pushed back.
- The user said their ideas "aren't perfect either. Feel free to push back." Do push back, with reasons.
- Inspiration came from the game Active Matter (Gaijin, 2026), but the goal is the principles, never copying its creatures.

## 2. Principles learned (master list)

**What makes a creature interesting**
1. **It bends something; its shape doesn't matter much.** The memorable ones twist a sense, a tool, a habit, or a fear the crew relies on. *(Source: my Active Matter picks were judged "normal". The user's Mimic inverted the noise economy.)*
2. **Strength and weakness come from one nature.** Watching it teaches both, and the counter always costs something.
3. **It attacks what the player trusts:** voices, the radio, alerts, a crewmate.
4. **The harm can be quiet:** "it doesn't hurt, until it does." The onset is felt but painless, so the player has to watch their own body.
5. **It makes its own conditions.** The Mimic is strongest when you're alone, and its voices create that loneliness.
6. **History without exposition.** "Who knows how long these things have lived" adds depth, and the traces carry it.
7. **A familiar label with a different mechanism underneath,** so the player's assumptions mislead them.
8. **Acting normal is fine if its beginning or end isn't.** The Reservoir sleepers fought like ordinary fast, hungry bodies. What made them memorable was the salt packing, waking when wet, and dissolving in water. *(The sleepers were cultist creations, but the principle applies to any threat.)*

**Ecology and plausibility**

9. **Its diet fits what the ruined world has plenty of:** the dead, war residue, Blood Crystals, ruin. Never something that's 99% gone. *(Source: my Sutures fed on medical supplies.)*
10. **A narrow habitat, ideally tied to its body.** Plentiful food is fine, but if the habitat is broad, the narrator decides it fits every drop and it crowds out variety. *(Source: my Mendicants v2 followed every battle site.)*
11. **Beasts are alive, not made.** They "eat", nest, grow, and compete. That's what separates them from cultist creations, which are made with a purpose and sent. A beast a cultist alters becomes a creation.
12. **They change by growing from what they take,** not by experimenting the way cultists do. One can change mid-drop when its own needs push it, but never just to cancel something clever the player did.

**Danger and readability**

13. **Danger is set per individual, not per species.** Age, growth, hunger, and how long it's been left alone decide where one sits. The same species can be a 3 or an 8.
14. **Growth is readable before it matters.** If a Cyno carries car-sized wreckage, it can probably lift a person.
15. **Know what it notices, and what it does then.** A creature that only watches isn't a threat yet. *(The same lesson as the Signaller role: passive information isn't a decision.)*
16. **A background threat must eventually touch the drop:** take things, change the site, or want what the crew needs. Otherwise it becomes scenery, as the ACC poles did.
17. **There's always a way to hurt it or drive it off with what a crew could have (but may not have).** A creature nobody can touch is never the only option.

**Presentation**

18. **Always describe appearance concretely** (size, silhouette, skin, movement, sound) so the player can picture it. In play, reveal it only as far as the character can see.
19. **Names:** no common real words that collide with ordinary talk (for example "Suture" in an injury-heavy game). Check them against names used in past runs too: Gleaner and harrow were already taken.
20. **The species name is for the narrator.** In-world names depend on who's talking, and each group keeps its own name for the whole run.

**Placement**

21. **Placement is level design, not a frequency rule.** Pick beast(s) because they fit the site AND make the drop more interesting.
22. **Plain fights are fine as part of a varied mix.** The C1 scenario (3 cultists in a building, pure skill) and the Threshing Field (mostly a combat test) are "normal," and that's acceptable. The issue is variety across drops.

## 3. Case: my Active Matter picks ("these look… normal")

**The ask:** which Active Matter enemies are the most noteworthy as unique distortions, to flesh out "the beasts of this ruined world," distinct from cultist abilities and from RWBY-style monsters.

**What I picked:** the Mimic Car (an object turned predator), the Flowermen (people overgrown, spreading), the Turned Soldiers (warped bodies that still use their training), the Distorted (lies dormant and rushes noise), and the Dendroids (trees with guns). My framing was "ordinary things warped, keeping a trace of what they were."

**The user's verdict:** "these look… normal." My picks were new shapes, but each played like a standard ambush or a standard monster.

**Lesson:** judge a creature by what it does to the game's systems and to the player's mind, not by how strange its body looks (principle 1).

## 4. Case: the Mimic (the user's design)

**The user's design.**
- **What it is:** not a traditional voice-stealing animal, but a black mass of lines. Think nanobots, "but not really": it must never read as technology, which would blur it with the ACC.
- **Cracks:** it shifts through even the smallest cracks.
- **Voices:** it produces the voices of living creatures and other sounds, but only ones it has heard. "Who knows how long these things have lived before your encounter with it."
- **Silence:** it "lives through a lack of noise." It's most effective, literally and psychologically, when you're alone.
- **Noise hurts it:** a gunshot damages it because of the sound, not the bullet.
- **How it harms:** it tries to assimilate someone, lines jolting into them. "It doesn't hurt, until it does."
- **Latching:** it can latch on to people.

**Why it works.**
- **It inverts the noise economy.** Everywhere else, shooting is punished. Here, noise is the cure, and the heat clock and responders are the price. Misses still hurt it, and a suppressed shot or a knife does little.
- **One nature, two sides:** silence is its strength and noise is its weakness, which makes it deducible from observation.
- **It makes its own conditions:** the voices split the crew up.
- **It turns safety tools into doubts:** crew voices, the handler, possibly a Warning module's beep.
- **Latching makes the strongest dilemma:** getting it off someone means making noise right beside them. That costs their hearing and heats the clock.

**Paths played out.**
- **Obedient crew sticking together:** safe only if the voices can't split them, and they can. It creates its own isolation.
- **Cautious path:** only stays costly if talking doesn't drive it off. The recommendation was that voices are its medium, and only sharp or loud noise hurts it.
- **Clever path:** an Alarm module becomes a weapon, an Explosive module a heavy one. The Rigger finally has a beast it's built for.
- **Fight:** it can be killed, but only with sustained noise, which costs ammo, a heat spike, and whatever answers.
- **Once understood:** the player fires at every untrusted voice. That burns ammo, draws threats, and one day the voice is a real crewmate. Understanding buys options, not safety.

**Guards identified.**
- **Noise as a hard counter.** Noise hurts it and drives it back, but it re-forms once things go quiet. The existing noise rules make constant noise expensive.
- **Knowledge leaks.** It only repeats what it has heard. Old voices belong to people who plausibly were at that site, come as fragments, and never carry exposition.
- **Assimilation without warning.** The onset is felt without pain: numbness, coldness. A suggested sign (mine): sound fading on the side being taken over.
- **Latching a crewmate onto the ship.** It reaches the ship only through signs that were on the page and missed, never in the background.
- **A latch that can't be undone.** Removal is always possible and always costly.
- **Vets explaining it.** No NPC yells "it hates noise." Plant the payoff before the first fight.

**Ratings:** perceived danger moderately high, actual 6–8 depending on the drop, psychological very high. It's worst on split-task drops and with a crew low on ammo.

**My suggestions (not confirmed by the user):** full assimilation means death, and the victim's voice joins what it can imitate. A latched person doesn't always know.

## 5. Case: the Cyno (the user's design)

**The user's design.**
- **What it is:** an orbiting thing in the sky, too far to shoot with pistols. Geometric things it picks up, like obelisk-shaped rocks, hover around it and shield it from shots.
- **What it notices:** it doesn't listen for noise or movement. It watches. It hovers high and moves on its own.
- **The pull:** it can try a gravitational pull in a small cone from where it's looking, pulling in objects to "grow" itself.
- **Cultists:** some know about these creatures. Some don't, and fear it or learn about it alongside the crew, especially those not capable of taking it down with their own long-range craft.
- **The ACC:** it can easily take them down, but only does so when it needs to. Until then it's a background threat. The user said: "Right now there's only the ACC poles, which get kind of boring after a while."
- **Change:** it can adapt and evolve.

**My pushback.**
1. **It risks becoming another ACC pole:** watching, unfightable, worked around. To stay interesting it has to touch the drop: take things, change the site (rubble opens and closes routes), and want what the crew needs.
2. **The miners can't fight it at all.** That's fine in the background but breaks "a hopeless option can't be the only fight" when it's the main problem. Suggestion: the Shooter's rifle reaches its shield and pistols don't.
3. **"Adapts and evolves" needs a beast meaning:** growing from what it takes, with its shape showing what it has absorbed, not experimenting like cultists.
4. **What happens when it sees you was undefined.** Does the pull work on people?
5. **The ACC can't be a rescue.** They kill it for their own reasons, and an ACC kill is its own danger.

**The user's answers.**
- **Danger:** "It's a low danger threat or high danger threat based on how it has evolved." Ones left alone a long time can eventually pull people. Difficulty is set case by case.
- **Name:** "Cyno" is the species name for narration. In-world names vary.
- **Frequency:** it's up to the narrator, used where appropriate: "whether this monster would fit the mission design AND be something that can help spice up the mission… It's like designing a level."

**My additions, flagged as invented, not confirmed:**
- **Bait:** steer its gaze by throwing junk into the open, or let it pull in a primed Explosive module.
- **Pods:** new pod landings are fresh metal and draw it.
- **The ACC:** it avoids places the ACC watches, so its position hints where they are.
- **Shield materials:** whatever is on site (rebar, car doors, bones), not always obelisks.

**Still open:** should the Cyno get a narrow habitat (principle 10)? The user's own variety concern applies to it. Options I offered: it needs rising air, it avoids ACC-watched ground, or it's territorial with one per wide region. The user's call.

## 6. Case: Sutures → Mendicants (my design, three versions)

**v1, "Sutures."** Dog-sized pale scavengers drawn to healing: scabs, dressings, antiseptic. They pick wounds open. It bends treatment: patching a crewmate makes them a target. The user's pushback:
1. **Name:** "suture" is a real word that comes up in injury talk.
2. **Appearance:** barely described, and the user couldn't picture them.
3. **Diet:** "Why would they adapt to feed on something that is already 99% gone from the desecrated world?"

**Diagnosis:** I built the creature around a mechanic I liked and then invented a diet to justify it. The world should come first, and the mechanic should grow out of it.

**v2, "Mendicants."** A rare real word meaning beggars, and they hover near the wounded like beggars.
- **Diet:** they graze young Blood Crystal growing out of old blood. In v4, crystals form from the residue of war.
- **Wounds:** they find crystal by the smell of blood setting hard, and a scabbing or dressed wound smells the same. That keeps the treatment dilemma, but now it comes from the diet. *(This says how they sense crystal, nothing about what crystals are.)*
- **Appearance:**
  - Long and low, the weight of a big dog.
  - Hairless skin the colour of old candle wax, crazed like cracked glaze.
  - Long front legs with splayed, jointed toes like a heron's; short back legs. They move tipped forward with the rump high, in bursts and then dead stillness.
  - An eyeless wedge head with a comb of translucent teeth underneath for scraping crystal.
  - Swallowed crystal glints red through the skin of the back. Older ones glitter more, so age is readable.
  - A dry rasp like a file on stone while grazing.

**The user's pushback on v2:** "It seems you're going to make the Mendicants a common monster." They followed fresh battle sites like miners do, so they fit almost every drop.

**v3, a narrow habitat.**
- **Where:** only where many people died indoors long ago: hospitals, shelters, bunkers, tunnels.
- **Behaviour:** sedentary. A nest grazes one site for years.
- **The body ties it in:** the cracked-glaze skin splits in wind and sun, so they need still, dark air. Open air hurts them over time, not instantly, and hunger can push them out briefly.
- **What meeting them says:** people died here, inside, long ago.

## 7. Case: RS-9 "The Relief Station" (an example drop)

The user asked for an example scenario, an Ashplan first drop. My first draft used a loose format, and the user supplied the KW-12 "Reservoir" debrief as the required format. That format is now written into the moderator prompt, section 6.

**At 4** (perceived 6, actual 4, psychological 5).
- **The site:**
  - A collapse-era relief clinic with one collapsed wing and a paved yard.
  - A crystal vein runs under it, shallow in the yard and rich in the basement cold store.
  - Wind in the yard masks short sounds. The basement air is still and carries sound up the stairwell.
  - Quota 6. The window opens 4h40 after touchdown and holds 40 minutes.
- **The actors:** a young Cyno that can't pull people and sweeps the yard of loose things; a nest of 4 Mendicants grazing the vein and the old blood under the building.
- **Signs:**
  - A glitter in the sky on descent.
  - A swept circle in the yard while the street outside is full of rubble.
  - Drip stands missing from their bolt marks.
  - Crystal knuckles grazed to grooved stumps, which is why the yard crystals come out cracked.
  - A grazed crystal bud on an old bloodstain, with the stain untouched.
  - **The planted observation:** a dead stray with a half-healed gash that has been eaten away while the rest of the body is untouched.
- **The turn:**
  1. Drilling exposes fresh crystal, and the smell draws the nest.
  2. The Cyno, feeding on the loose chips, peels up the asphalt and exposes the whole shallow vein. The yard becomes a feeding ground, with the nest between the crew and the pod.
- **End stretch:** the extraction pod is fresh metal, and the Cyno tugs at its fittings.
- **Paths:**
  1. **The yard:** fast, cracked crystals, one person stuck guarding gear.
  2. **The basement:** rich, beside the nest, one stairwell out.
  3. **The collapsed wing:** unseen by both, slow, 1–2 crystals short.
  4. **The storm drain:** intact crystals; cramped and wet; the grate is in the open yard; one way out.
- **Clever paths:** bait the nest, feed the Cyno junk, leave wounds undressed until the nest is gone.
- **Unexplained:** an old pod hatch with a Sunspot stencil in the Cyno's ring; a logbook that stops mid-sentence.

**At 8.**
- A mature Cyno that can drag a person a few metres; a hungry colony of about 10 older Mendicants.
- Still air. The window opens 4h10 after touchdown and holds 25 minutes.
- **New signs:** drag marks toward the centre of the swept circle; an old crew's satchel strap three metres up a lamp post.
- **The turn:** the Cyno pulls the clinic's whole front down and buries the only stairwell out of the basement.
- **New unusual path:** the roof, where a rifle can reach the Cyno's shield, at the cost of being seen.

**At 11** (only through an earned story or a death mission; the stacking rules changed later).
- An old Cyno that lifts people and drops them, with a ring the size of a house; a starving colony hunting the wounded.
- **A third actor:** the ACC, there to kill the Cyno because it threatens their drone lanes. *(The drone-lane motive is invented lore.)*
- **The turn:** the ACC kills it, and everything in its ring falls across the yard.
- **Signs:** a drone circling, and the ring pulling tighter beforehand. The warning is brief.
- **Escalation:** Peacekeepers come down to confirm the kill.
- **Unusual path:** under the living Cyno, the one place the ACC won't fire until they commit.

**The user's notes on the scaling:**
- **Walk-ins are always an option:** cultists spotting a Cyno and wanting it for their craft, another beast approaching out of curiosity, or something else. Keep the actors active at once few.
- **A bigger quota** can raise difficulty by forcing two or more spots. It's set before the drop and never raised mid-drop.
- **A mid-drop pay raise** happens when Sunspot underestimated the drop, something evolved or behaved in a way it didn't account for, or something significant walked in.
- **"Stay in the pod and wait"** is never listed as a path. It's always the safest and always earns no quota. The last path is an unusual route, and it isn't always the best one.
- None of these are required. "You don't always HAVE to do this."

## 8. Related decisions from the same work

- **Cultists and beasts:** "wants to experiment on it" was rejected as too simple; it undersells cultists. Cultists who know a species can make it part of their craft: material, a tool, bait, or a technique built around its nature.
- **The Reservoir wasn't a puzzle:** the user didn't experience it as one. Its rule read as part of the world, which is the goal.
- **Drop length** varies as level design: usually 6+ hours when threats are meant to be learned, shorter for pressure or straight-fight drops.
- **No summarising:** the user rejected both "something meaningful each hour" (arbitrary) and narrator summarising. "It's the narrator's job to keep things moving, not skip over the boring things."
- **Eating in quotes:** the BEASTS section writes "eat" in quotes, because eating can mean pulling in, absorbing, or assimilating.
- **"Usually winnable" was cut** from the beasts section. Beasts range by individual, from easy to overwhelming.

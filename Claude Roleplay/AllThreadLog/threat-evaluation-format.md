# Coreworld IV: Standard Threat Evaluation Format

Use this format for every drop evaluation, in any playthrough. One evaluation per drop. Spoilers are expected: these are written after the drop, out of character, for design review.

---

## Template

```
# Drop N: SITE-CODE, "Short Name"

| Perceived | Actual (as designed) | Actual (as played) | Psychological |
|---|---|---|---|
| x | x | x | x |

**The site.** Layout and points of interest: landing spot, distances, terrain, weather and wind, sound carry. Window timing and quota.

**What it really was.** Each actor and what it needs or wants. Hidden rules written as tendencies, not laws. What the crew was walking into.

**Signs, blended into normal description.** Every sign that was on the page before it mattered, including the one in-passing reaction planted before a fight.

**The turn.** What changed partway through, its in-world cause, and how it could be seen and answered.

**Escalation.** (Optional) How noise, choices and the heat clock stacked up.
```

## Field notes

**Ratings (1–10, plus "Past 10" when it applies)**
- **Perceived:** how dangerous it felt to play. It should never sit below Actual, but it can point at the wrong thing.
- **Actual (as designed):** the prompt's danger scale, as the drop was built for a crew of 4 with normal preparation, after modes, formats and the intake answer are applied.
- **Actual (as played):** what really happened at the table. If this is lower than the design, the grading section must say why.
- **Psychological:** dread, moral weight, the personal and the unknown, separate from physical danger. If the player gives their own rating, use it and say why the narrator's differed.

**Paths not taken**
- Prioritize route-level forks: choices that change the shape of the whole drop (where to base, who to ally with or fight, what to risk, when to accept a loss). Tactical alternatives (how to open a door, which stone to pry) only belong here if they would have changed the route.
- Aim for 4–6 routes. Each one should describe the drop as it would have played out, not a single action.
- Judge each route only on what the crew knew when the fork opened. Never grade it on information they didn't have.
- Outcomes are the narrator's best read of the drop as built. Mark them "likely" and never present them as certain.
- Every route must meet the turn. If the turn would have skipped a route entirely, say so and flag it in Grading as a design weakness.
- If a route would have been strictly better at no real cost, say so plainly and flag it in Grading.
- Include the cautious route (holding back, going home short) and show its cost, since the cautious path is never free.
- Routes can be spoiler-light. Hold back anything still in play without saying something is held back, except under "Paths you couldn't see."

**Rules for writing an evaluation**
- Base claims on what happened in play. Quote lines or numbers where they matter.
- Name every narrator mistake plainly (retcons, wrong counts, knowledge leaks, NPCs solving, softening), not just design weaknesses.
- Check the drop against the core design rules:
  - threats aren't puzzles, and no safe zone should come from understanding one;
  - the turn comes from cause and effect;
  - the cautious path isn't free;
  - clever play keeps what it earned;
  - nobody solves a threat for the player;
  - nothing is softened for a hurt or small crew.
- Write the player's moves as they did them, and their results, without judging the player.
- Mark anything still undecided as undecided. Never invent an explanation after the fact.

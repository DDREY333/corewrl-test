# Drop 1: GT-9, "The Grain Terminal"

*The player's comment after both drops: "honestly didnt feel like 8s." The narrator's first ratings were 8 / 7 / 8 / 8 and were revised down. This drop was run by a different session. **The site.** An old grain terminal on the east bank of a slow river: fog, still air, and sound that carries a long way.
- **Pod:** landed in the front loading yard among rail lines and pale weeds. A row of seven hopper cars stands about 20 m away. The first car has a glittering smear of pale flakes from knee to chest height.
- **The clean strip:** a 2 m strip of bare, mirror-clean concrete runs across the yard beside an old scorch streak.
- **The weigh-house:** a squat brick building about 50 m to the right, with one dark window, a door that was shut at landing, and a scale plate in front.
- **The silos:** six or more in a row, about 40 m away.
  - **Silo 3** has a chest-high blast hole in its front with a nine-day-old cultist corpse beside it. Its back has a long crack with a pale crust running down from it, and a half-open discharge chute.
  - **Silo 4** has a rusted door.
- **The gantry** runs between silos 2 and 3, with a drip and a crusted puddle beneath it. Its maintenance ladder is cut off clean about 2.5 m up.
- **The back apron** runs down to the river: pilings, a half-sunk barge, mud and reeds.
- **Window:** opened 6 h after touchdown, held 40 min. **Quota 4,** at 30 a head and 3 per intact past quota. Regraded mid-drop to 45 and 5.

**What it really was** *(reconstructed)*. Two actors, with the site as a third.
- **The Glassman** (cultist practitioner):
  - He grows "glass" creatures by tuning red crystal with sound. He carried a two-tined bone fork that rings the same note as the silo, a vial of pale flakes, and two red chips.
  - His creature was a dog-sized crawler of frosted-glass plates, grown "clear" on crystal song. It nested high in silo 3 and reacted to crystal hum and to ringing metal. It went for the crystal, not for people.
  - He watched from the weigh-house. He had watched pod drops before: *"Same boxes. Same little numbers. Same Rigger hands."*
  - What he wanted: the red the miners carry, so he could *"put a little song inside it, and listen where it goes."* When his glass died, he went for the pod.
- **The ACC** (a collection cycle on the nine-day-old battlefield):
  - **The Ticker:** a survey drone. Slate-grey, hangs point-down, spins, and makes an identical dry tick. It flies straight lines at walking pace and sweeps a flat light from a slot in its side. It can't see straight below itself. When it narrows its light to a point, that point is a mark.
  - **The Throb:** the carrier.
  - **The Hisser:** a flat collector sheet with a white light-edge. It erases what lies on the ground, including the dead and, by rumour, red. It spared walls and the pod hull.
  - The clean strip was an earlier collection pass.
- **The site:** fog, still air, and one exposed pod with an old-model hatch whose hinge pins sit on the outside.
- **Never explained:** the bare footprints coming up from the reeds (presumably the Glassman), the single click to the east, the crow calls.

**Signs, blended into normal description:**
- The clean strip. Sol: *"Somebody tidied up."* Later: *"Saw one of these my second drop… There'd been bodies in that square."*
- The glitter smear on the first hopper (the crawler rubbing against it). Pale crust under the gantry drip and down the silo crack. Pale flecks drifting down out of the fog.
- The gantry ladder cut off clean, with flared stubs.
- **The planted reaction:** while clamping the Shock wire, Enn knocked the rebar and it rang. A moment later a thin, clear note came down from high in silo 3, the same note. That was the glass answering a ring.
- A single ring in the second quiet between drill bursts, which Sol didn't hear.
- The strong hum from the big slab crystal, and flakes falling from the crack. A longer ring, which Sol did hear.
- Bare footprints out of the reeds by silo 5, and the click to the east.
- The weigh-house door: shut at landing, then a hand's width open, then wide open.
- The Ticker's tick, identical every time, and its flat sweeping light.

**The turn.** After the crew killed his glass and walked back to the pod with quota, the Glassman crossed the yard and climbed in through the hatch they'd left open. He tied the inner wheel to a seat anchor with a cut harness strap and went to work on the beacon with a bone shard.
- **Its cause:** they killed what he was growing, they carried the red, and they left their only way home standing open.
- **How it could be seen:** the door opening in stages, the watcher at silo 5's corner, the figure stepping out of the weigh-house.
- **How it could be answered:** drill out the outside hinge pins (Dorsey: *"the old model was a bad idea"*); wait while he bled out from Enn's hit; or the deal, which was a lie.

**Escalation.**
1. The drill noise and four gunshots brought the Ticker: three passes.
2. On the third pass it hovered over the pod and swept the cabin. The light stopped a hand's width short of the crew, who were in the blind spot under the porthole.
3. The pin drilling brought a fourth pass. It narrowed to a point on the open hinge crack and left a clean coin of bare steel.
4. The Throb came down past the river, and the Hisser came out.
5. The Hisser erased the old corpse at silo 3, then turned toward the Glassman's body by the pod.
6. Its light edge came through the gap left at the hinge seam and took the foil tape, Sol's toecap, and the drill's bit and chuck.

## The paths you didn't take

Ordered by when each fork opened. "Known then" lists only what the crew had seen by that point. Outcomes are the narrator's best read of the drop as built, not certainties.

### Route A: The front-hole pair first
- **The fork:** after the first crystal. Sol offered it: *"Those two are right there. Easy cuts… Or we walk the base."*
- **Known then:** one ring from high in silo 3; the body by the hole; the Shock wire across the hole.
- **The shape of it:** two small cuts kneeling in the mulch, inside silo 3, directly under whatever rang. Quota 3 within twenty minutes, then a short trip round the back for the fourth.
- **Likely cost:** drilling inside the glass's own silo. The hum and the noise go straight up the shaft to it. The crawler likely comes down the inside wall at Sol in the dark, with your wire behind her.
- **How the turn meets it:** sooner. The glass dies, or wins, earlier and inside the silo, and the Glassman starts on the pod earlier.
- **What it would have bought:** speed, and the front kept close to the pod.
- **Versus as played:** Worse, likely. A fight in the dark at arm's length.

### Route B: Leave the slab for good
- **The fork:** when the slab sang. Sol: *"Walk now."* Later you went back for a "fast one."
- **Known then:** a strong hum; flakes falling; a ring Sol also heard; bare tracks east; a click.
- **The shape of it:** silo 5's crystal, then the front hole for the thumb, never touching the slab again. Quota at the front.
- **Likely cost:** the crawler is still alive. It wants crystal, so it comes to the front-hole drilling instead, inside the silo. Sol keeps both arms, maybe.
- **How the turn meets it:** the Glassman still has his glass. He may hold back and watch, or send it after the red you're carrying. Undecided.

- **Versus as played:** A trade. An unhurt Driller against a threat left unresolved, close to the pod.

### Route C: Stay sealed in the pod after the Ticker's hover (the cautious route)
- **The fork:** after the light swept the cabin. Sol: *"My head says the box is the only way up."* You chose to get out.
- **Known then:** the light reached into the cabin but couldn't reach directly below the porthole; the open weigh-house door; Sol's rumours (Hesk sat it out and lived; Marrow's crew was caught digging; a pod left fine with its crew gone).

# Drop 2: CR-11, "The Burning Refinery"

| Perceived | Actual (as designed) | Actual (as played) | Psychological |

**The site.** A burning refinery on a flat coastal plain under heavy yellow-brown smoke lit orange from inside. The roar of the fires swallows a lot of noise. Wind comes off the sea from the east in cold gusts that tear the smoke open.
- **Pod 4:** landed on tacky tar concrete. A burning pool lies 20 m out, toward the towers.
- **The cracking towers:** four, ahead and to the left. Tower 2 leans and booms as it shifts. Tower 3 has a south door and a north door.
- **The pump house:** brick, with a tin roof, about 40 m to the right. Behind it, a still-air alley.
- **The tank farm:** to the right, toward the sea. A chest-high wall surrounds it, with an oil lake inside, burning in patches. A grated walkway runs over the lake, there's a breach in the wall, and steps go over it.
- **The east end:** cold and clean, with salt in the cracks. A torn-open tank there has red along its welds. The sea is about 200 m beyond.
- **The ground between:** pipe racks, a CONTROL block, a burnt tanker lying on its side, a battle crater with mirror-cut scorch lines, a drainage ditch with pipe bridges. The flare stack stands to the south.
- **Window:** opened 5 h after touchdown, held 30 min. **Quota 3,** at 40 a head and 6 per intact past quota. Regraded mid-drop to 55 and 8.
- **Briefing:** reduced. Handler Cassel. Smoke; filters *"adequate for some hours"*; crystals at tower 3's base and the east end of the tanks; *"There is another pod on site."*

**What it really was.** The site, plus three actors with their own needs and a fourth crew already down.
- **The pall** (a beast; the name comes from Sunspot reports):
  - A vast, thin, translucent sheet that rides the smoke's updrafts and feeds on heat. It comes down on the strongest heat nearby and stays until that heat is cold, leaving a frost ring.
  - It ignores sound. Living people are safe from it only while something warmer is nearby or while their heat is masked. It will take a person when they're the warmest thing around.
- **The coat men** (cultists):
  - They wear coats of pall skin, which hide their heat, so they walk under it "like weather." A punctured or dead wearer leaks heat, visible as threads of steam, and draws it down.
  - They harvest red, and they carry person-sized bundles wrapped in pall skin to tower 3. Pod two's driller, Addo, is in one; her boots show.
  - A practitioner in tower 3 works the hides. The crew heard only a slow scraping, *"like a blade drawn flat along a stretched hide,"* and it didn't react to the drill, the gunfire or the explosion. This was **intended but never shown.**
  - Their weapon is a sharpened hooked rib. They signal with a two-note breathy whistle and come looking when their own go quiet.
- **The ACC:** ambient survey traffic over the coast. A big noise draws the comb (the Ticker), which marks what it wants. The carrier and collector then clear the marked site. Traffic rises after the explosion and brings a **survey team** (synchronized steps, a hum, a blue line traced round the hatch seam), its first appearance in this playthrough. Cassel described that tell in advance.
- **The site:**
  - New-batch paper filters (rims 40 and up) have no charcoal layer and fail in smoke. Pod two's 43 and 44 had gone brown-black in about 4 h.
  - Smoke can false-trip beams.
  - Tower 2 is failing and collapsed during the drop.
  - Cold drains a pod's cell, and the grey "C" box in a red pod draws on that same cell.
- **Pod two** (Dorsey's crew, white slip): touched down about 4 h earlier. Addo was taken on her way to their pod for a trauma pouch. Varga broke his leg falling from the walkway when the pall came down beside him, and sat tapping a pistol on the rail for hours. Pod two's window closed while he waited.

**Signs, blended into normal description:**
- **The pall:**
  - The burning pool beside the pod went out *"as if someone had put a lid on it"* right after touchdown.
  - Patches of oil inside the wall went dark, and the smoke above them "sagged."
  - Holt: *"The fires keep going out."*
  - The flame on top of a tank went down all at once.
- **Varga's account:** *"Comes down on a fire and sits on it till it's out… Never came down on us. Only fire… Coat man walked right under it."*
- **Cassel's reports:** a flare; fires burned low; *"rare, and complete when it happens."*
- **The warm box,** noted at strap-in and again at touchdown.
- **Filters:** pod two's brown #43 on the top step, with prints going out and none coming back. Okafor's rim lesson on the ship, and Dov's "lighter crate."
- **The coat men:** the tinking, a dragged foot, a tar handprint. Two-note whistles that answer each other.
- **The planted reaction before the first fight:** at 60 m both coat men stopped dead and lifted their coats over their heads when a gust cleared the smoke. It happened again with the trio before the explosion.
- **Tower 3:** the six bundles, Addo's boots, and the scraping.
- **The ACC:** the comb's identical tick after the explosion; the narrowed point on the blast site; the Throb, then the hiss, then a "tearing canvas" sound; Cassel's *"traffic… gone up"*; the survey team's steps, exactly as he had described them.
- **Tower 2:** the bong, then a grating falling 15 m from the crew.

**The turn.** With the fires burning lower and the ground cold, the pall found the strongest steady heat left: the warm grey box in pod 4. It draped over the pod, froze the hatch wheel, and drained the cell.
- **Its cause:** its hunger, plus the box's draw. Both were on the page beforehand.
- **How it could be seen:** the pod's shape gone wrong, a frost ring on the tar, and no Warning tone, because it worked from the outside.
- **How it could be answered:** a hotter source somewhere else, as Cassel's reports said (flares, burning tar, steaming bodies); the crowbar on a frozen wheel; Rigger work on the cell.

**Escalation.**
1. The drill inside the steel tank rang like a bell and drew the coat men's whistles.
2. The two-man fight left steam threads from the bullet holes, which drew the pall off the fires.
3. The Explosive wire drew three more coat men, set an oil fire, and killed the trio. It also brought the comb, which marked the blast site and called the carrier and collector.
4. ACC traffic rose and brought the survey team.
5. The pump-house fight left two more steaming bodies, which pulled the pall off the pod onto the hut.
6. Three people in a two-seat pod with a cold cell meant thin air and a flickering beacon.

## Grading myself

**What worked:**
- **The pall was readable.** Lids on fires, sagging smoke, Varga's account, and Cassel's reports all came before it mattered. Its heat rule could be worked out from what you saw.
- **The planted reaction was on the page before the first fight** (coats over heads in the gust). It was shown twice and never explained.
- **The turn grew from the pall's own need** and a fact you'd been shown: the warm box.
- **Cassel stayed muddy:** honest about limits, free with partial answers, and quietly unsettling (*"Teeth together, eyes open,"* *"Understood"*).
- **The Rigger mattered:** Warning wires gave real alerts; the Explosive ambush; the cell repair.
- **The ACC escalated by tier** and kept its tells: comb, carrier, collector, then the first survey team, whose tell was given in advance.
- **Pod two's fate gave weight and choices** (Varga, Addo, the satchel deal).

**What didn't:**
1. **The coat men fought dumb.** The prompt says enemies "fight smart and dirty," and these walked open ground into pistol range and charged armed miners with melee weapons. The roof cut was the one real "strike through walls," and even it ended in a frontal rush. They never used the pall, the smoke, the bundles, Varga as bait, or your predictable return to the pod. The player was right.
2. **The pall had one cheap answer.** Flares solved it every time, and it never went for a living crew member. Under the new cost rule, flares plus pistols together leave the drop costing almost nothing, so the drop wasn't finished.
3. **The turn was undone by your own fight.** Steaming bodies pulled the pall off the pod before it cost you anything. That was cause and effect, but it resolved too neatly.
4. **The ACC never forced a costly choice.** It marked, collected and scanned. "Stay still and dark" was always free.
5. **Sized for two.** CR-11 was built knowing the crew was two, which the new crew-size rule now forbids. The design 8 was reached through what the threats could do, not through what good play would cost.
6. **Varga said "tin"** (your crew's private word). An accidental echo, fixed out of character.
7. **Named Bryn "Vasse"** in narration before anyone had named her. Struck.
8. **Caps arithmetic:** after paying Wendel, the balance was shown as 43 instead of 42. Fixed in Ledger #2.
9. **Costs landed on others.** Pod two paid most of this drop's price. The crew's costs were Caps and consumables, which the new cost rule doesn't count.

# Drop 1: KW-12, "The Reservoir"

| Perceived | Actual (as designed) | Actual (as played) | Psychological |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 9 | 7 | 8 |

**The site.** A drained reservoir below a hydro dam, with a drowned stone town on the silt bed. The ACC took the dam's crest two days before the crew landed. Fresh crystals grow from the dead of that fight.
- The pod landed in the town square.
- A stepped street drops to the bottom street beside the river channel.
- A church stands on a hump to the east.
- There's a crystal mass at the dam's outlet pipe.
- An old abandoned Sunspot pod sits on the flats, its beacon blinking.
- The reservoir bed is a bowl of still air, so sound carries a long way.
- Window: 4h30 after touchdown, held for 30 minutes. Quota 5.

**What it really was.** Three actors with their own goals, none of them about the crew.
- **The ACC** closed the dam to refill the reservoir. That was the clock.
- **The Brinesleep sleepers** belong to a practitioner, Hollow Anselm, who was never seen. He packs the dead and the nearly dead with salt and bone meal and leaves them inert. Water wakes them. They're fast, hungry, and move toward noise. A head shot blinds them, a hip shot cripples them, and soaking slowly dissolves them into a milky cloud. He laid them out facing the dam so the rising water would send them up against the ACC garrison. The crew landed inside his trap.
- **The water** was the timer for both of them.

**Signs, blended into normal description:**
- Some bodies were neat: facing the dam, coats dusty and dry, white crust at the mouth. The rest were tangled where they fell, damp, with flies on them.
- When Pall lay among the neat ones, his neighbour's coat was dry and smelled of dust instead of rot, and the body felt packed, like a sack of grain.
- Pall's dent in the silt was wet. The neat body left no damp mark.
- More bodies lay in the ditch, and Teo said they "jumped".
- Anselm's signature, before he arrived: salt on the tongue, and a dry rattle like a gourd.

**The turn.** In two parts:
- The water reached the ditch, and the first sleeper folded up.
- The boxes met over the pipe and sealed it, and the slow rise broke into a fast spread across the flats. That was a learned rule breaking, with a visible cause.

**Escalation.** Each step drew the next:
- The drill, the rifle, a pistol shot at the dam, and the ramp slamming brought the drone back.
- The drone narrowed its light and marked the pod with a ring.
- The box walked down to the pod.
- Anselm arrived and fought the box; salt crust climbed its legs and its rhythm stumbled.
- The box called three Peacekeepers.
- Two were still on the ground at launch, and one watched the pod go.

## The paths you didn't take

Ordered by when each fork opened. "Known then" lists only what the crew had seen by that point. Outcomes are the narrator's best read of the drop as built, not certainties.

### Route A: Seal the pod and wait out the window
- **The fork:** at landing, before anyone left the pod. The site raised it: the briefing's quota versus a dead town full of bodies. Teo argued for it later, after the wake.
- **Known then:** a dam crest held by the ACC that "have not looked down yet"; water "returning... slow"; an abandoned pod on the flats with its hatch open; quota 5 at 50 each.
- **The shape of it:** four people in a closed pod for 4h30, listening. There's no drill noise and no gunfire. The drone pass goes over a quiet square.
- **Likely cost:** quota missed entirely, so every crew member takes a shortfall debt and a worse next assignment. The water still comes: by window time it's at the hatch seam and the launch burn is under water. Sitting in a metal can with no view.
- **How the turn meets it:** the same, and blind. The fast spread reaches the square sooner than anyone inside could judge. The sleepers on the bottom street still wake when the flood reaches them, and with nothing to draw them, they likely head for the dam, not the pod.
- **What it would have bought:** probably no ring, since nothing drew the drone back. Teo uninjured. Nobody marked. The cost lands on later drops instead: debt, and the board's opinion of Pod 31.
- **Versus as played:** A trade. Safer bodies now, a poorer and worse-placed crew later, and the same launch through water.

### Route B: Rifle on the roof with Sabe
- **The fork:** Odile's question at the square: "Where do you want to be?" You raised it ("Overwatch? Or with you?"), then deferred to her.
- **Known then:** a scout on the north roof; bodies everywhere; the dam crest with a "box" on it; Odile's warning that the rifle without glass "guesses at shapes" past a couple of hundred.
- **The shape of it:** Pall and Sabe on two roofs over the square and the top of the stepped street. Odile and Teo drill the bottom street alone, with no ears on the open end.
- **Likely cost:** when the first sleeper climbs the channel wall, nobody at street level sees it before it's on Odile. Lying flat on a roof during the drone pass is more exposed than lying among the dead. There's no guard at the stair head, and a much harder shot down the stepped street.
- **How the turn meets it:** harder for the two at the bottom, easier for the two on top. You'd see the water spread from above, earlier and more clearly.
- **What it would have bought:** a better view of the boxes, the flood, and the sleepers heading for the dam. Possibly an earlier decision to leave the low town.
- **Versus as played:** Worse, likely. It would have saved your view and cost Odile or Teo their margin at the wake.

### Route C: The church first
- **The fork:** Sabe's roof report of three deposits; Odile chose the bottom street.
- **Known then:** "Lots of bodies. Big pieces" at the bottom; "small, not many" at the church; a big lump at the pipe "with their stuff all round it"; the water "lower than the steps. For now."
- **The shape of it:** drilling small, poor crystals on a dry hilltop, with the drill noise carrying over the whole town. Quota would be very hard: two small pieces there, and three more would have to come from the low town later.
- **Likely cost:** quota short, or a late trip down into the low town just as the flood spreads. Every sleeper there would wake at once, between you and the pod.
- **How the turn meets it:** later and worse. The fast spread arrives while you're still short, and the wake is a crowd instead of one sleeper at a time.
- **What it would have bought:** height, distance, and time to watch the water rule play out on someone else.
- **Versus as played:** Worse. The bottom street's early ugliness was the cheapest the low town would ever be.

### Route D: The dam outlet
- **The fork:** the same report: a "really big" lump at the pipe.
- **Known then:** it sits at the foot of the dam, under the crest, with broken ACC wreckage around it.
- **The shape of it:** one long drill job right under the boxes. Quota from a single mass, fast.
- **Likely cost:** drilling noise directly below the ACC. When the sleepers climbed the dam face, they'd climb right over you, and the boxes' fire would land on the face where you're working.
- **How the turn meets it:** directly. The water and the sleepers converge on exactly where you are.
- **What it would have bought:** the most crystal for the least walking, and possibly over-quota pay.
- **Versus as played:** Worse. The highest chance of a death on the drop.

### Route E: All four go down to the pod together after quota
- **The fork:** after the fifth crystal and the boom, when Odile asked your call on the hatch: "Two go, or everyone goes."
- **Known then:** water spreading fast across the flats; the hatch left open; sleepers wading toward the dam; Teo can't run well.
- **The shape of it:** one group through the arch and down the lane together. Four guns, not one.
- **Likely cost:** the arch is still the choke point, and the tall blind one is still in it. Without the radio call, it may not lock onto Teo. The ramp still needs two to lift. The noise of closing is the same.
- **How the turn meets it:** the same, but with nobody left on a hill and nobody alone.
- **What it would have bought:** probably no shoulder wound for Teo, no split crew, and an earlier seal.
- **Versus as played:** Better, likely. It wasn't free, but it beat the split.

### Paths you couldn't see
- **Using the water against the sleepers:** keep them dry, or drown them deliberately and let them dissolve. The crew only saw "milk" round the soaked ones late, in the square.
- **Letting the ACC handle them:** make noise near the dam face so the sleepers climb into the boxes' fire, which is what their maker intended.

## Grading myself

**What worked:**
- The sleeper rule could be worked out from what you saw.
- The water breaking its slow rise had a visible cause.
- Odile acted like a veteran.
- Nobody solved the threat for you.
- The pouch reveal was an honest consequence.
- The countdown was compressed once you were in the pod.

**What didn't:**
1. **Actual danger landed around 7, not 9.** Nobody died, the quota was met, and the Peacekeepers let you go.
2. **The planted reaction came late.** The sleeper turning toward the drill happened as it woke, not earlier in passing.
3. **A garbled line:** "Your palm throbs... No, it's only a bruise."
4. **Misleading ammo wording:** "Twelve rounds in the pistol" meant 5 plus a spare magazine of 7. Later the magazine was miscounted as six, when it was five in the magazine plus one chambered. This was corrected out of character.
5. **The water rose faster than my own notes,** without the pace being shown early enough.
6. **The lever and inside winch were invented on the spot** when the scene needed them.

## What stays unexplained
- The old pod: open hatch, empty seats, light still blinking.
- The ring on the hatch. **Undecided by the narrator.** Pod 19 (empty) and Pod 8 (powdered) also came back ringed. Why Pod 31 was spared is unknown.
- Why the Peacekeeper touched the ring and let the pod launch.
- Anselm himself: the laugh, the sigh, and the rattle that stopped mid-shake.
- The deep hum. It came back at TN-6.
- The second box on the crest, which never moved.

# Drop 2: TN-6, "The Threshing Field"

| Perceived | Actual (as designed) | Actual (as played) | Psychological |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 8 (should have been 9 or more; see grading) | 7 | 8 |

**The site.** A dead grain plain where the stalks stand a head taller than a man, with a constant west wind.
- **North:** three silos joined by a gantry. One stair has a three-step gap, and the far silo has a back ladder.
- **East:** a rail embankment with derailed boxcars.
- **Centre:** the ACC burn ring, a 150 m disc of glassed black ground, with crystals thick along its edge in the burned cultists.
- **Southwest:** the pod, about 400 m from the ring, in grass.
- Window: 5h after touchdown, held for 40 minutes. Quota 4.

**What it really was.** Three actors with their own needs.
- **The harrows.** A native pack of beasts that bleed and can be killed. They track movement and vibration through grass, and in daylight they tend to stay off bare ground. Their den was under the third boxcar: a wounded mother and four young.
- **The ACC harvest.** This was the turn: the ACC collecting crystals. A lifter hung over the ring, with threads and rakes. Its sweep lights pushed outward in a growing circle and herded the harrows ahead of them, toward the silos and the pod.
- **The grass.** It blinds the scout and the rifle at once.

**Signs, blended into normal description:**
- A patch of stalks leaning into the wind at the burn circle.
- Dry ticking under the hiss.
- The planted reaction: the drill clanged on an iron wheel, and a ripple froze, ran, then drifted back.
- Stalks shivering in time with the drill.
- At the rail, weak ticking, and the young answering it.

**The turn.** The deep hum (the same one as KW-12), then the slab, the threads, and the sweep. Being herded was the new problem. Getting behind the sweep was the answer you could see.

## The paths you didn't take

Ordered by when each fork opened. "Known then" lists only what the crew had seen by that point. Outcomes are the narrator's best read of the drop as built, not certainties.

### Route A: Silos first
- **The fork:** Odile's vote at the edge of the pod's burn circle. Sabe and Ruhl voted ring.
- **Known then:** grass taller than a man; silos 600 m north; the ring 400 m northeast with "red... lots" along its edge; the rail further east; one patch of stalks leaning against the wind.
- **The shape of it:** a longer walk through the grass to height first. You'd watch the field from the gantry, then walk out to the ring or the rail for crystals.
- **Likely cost:** a longer first crossing with the pack following, and the walk from the silos to the crystals is through the grass again. You'd likely be starting at the ring later, closer to the turn.
- **How the turn meets it:** sooner. You'd see the slab coming from the gantry, but you'd be short on crystals when the sweep starts, and the herded pack arrives at the silos while you're still on them.
- **What it would have bought:** an early, clear read of the harrows' movement from above, before any fight.
- **Versus as played:** Mixed. Better information early, but less time at the crystals before the turn.

### Route B: The rail first
- **The fork:** the same vote. Nobody picked it.
- **Known then:** H.'s briefing that there might be dead in the boxcars; the rail further than the ring, with grass all the way.
- **The shape of it:** the longest first crossing, to the boxcars. Steel walls, a raised embankment, and dead inside with crystals.
- **Likely cost:** you'd find the den before any fight, at close range, with the mother still able to stand, so a much worse first contact. Drilling in a boxcar rings like a bell.
- **How the turn meets it:** head on. The slab crosses the rail first, coming from the east toward the ring, so the sweep starts with you directly under it.
- **What it would have bought:** the embankment's height and steel cover.
- **Versus as played:** Worse.

### Route C: One more crystal at the ring before leaving
- **The fork:** after the first pack fight, when the scope showed the slab coming. Odile: "One more, or we go now, with three."
- **Known then:** five harrows dead, one fled east; something "flat, dark, enormous" coming west with threads under it; the crystals at the edge are in reach.
- **The shape of it:** one more drilling job on the glass with the slab closing, then leave.
- **Likely cost:** the drill noise brings the pack's survivors back in. The rakes and the threads reach the ring edge while you're still on it. If the sweep starts with you on the glass, it pushes the pack straight at you on open ground.
- **How the turn meets it:** directly. You'd be in the slab's working area when it arrived.
- **What it would have bought:** quota met at the ring. No rail trip, and no den.
- **Versus as played:** Unknown. A tighter, more dangerous half hour in exchange for skipping the rail entirely.

### Route D: Hide inside the silo
- **The fork:** in the silo yard, when you asked for "chokepoints". Odile rejected it: "One door. No."
- **Known then:** a half-open iron hatch, dark inside, smelling of old grain "and something sweeter"; the stair has a gap; the gantry was untested.
- **The shape of it:** the crew inside a dark concrete cylinder with one doorway, waiting for the sweep to pass.
- **Likely cost:** the pack is herded into the yard by the sweep, with you behind one door. If something inside was the source of that sweeter smell, you'd find out in the dark.
- **How the turn meets it:** worse. You'd be herded into a corner and unable to see the sweep pass.
- **What it would have bought:** walls, and maybe being unseen by the light.
- **Versus as played:** Worse.

### Route E: Straight to the pod with three, from the gantry
- **The fork:** on the gantry, when the sweep started and Odile said "I'm out of good ideas."
- **Known then:** three crystals; the sweep coming outward; the pack being herded ahead of it, toward the pod; Teo's drop showed what shortfall costs.
- **The shape of it:** down the back ladder and 600 m southwest through the grass, the cautious route home.
- **Likely cost:** quota short, so debt for all four and worse sites. The sweep is pushing the pack toward the pod at the same time as you walk to it. You'd likely arrive at the pod mid-fight, in the grass, with the hatch open.
- **How the turn meets it:** directly. You'd be walking into the herded pack.
- **What it would have bought:** the drill kept, and Odile spared the 90-Cap debt.
- **Versus as played:** Worse. The cautious path here walked into the turn.

### Route F: Spare the den
- **The fork:** at the rail. Ruhl: "Not those." Odile: kill them now, or "next crew that drops here, there's twelve."
- **Known then:** a wounded mother "dark all down one side", unable to stand, with four young; the crystal in the next car.
- **The shape of it:** drill the second car anyway, and leave the den alone under the third.
- **Likely cost:** the mother can't rise. The young are too small to hunt. The drill noise is the same either way. Nothing worse happens on this drop, likely.
- **How the turn meets it:** it doesn't change it.
- **What it would have bought:** Ruhl's line held, and Odile spared that act. The pack's next generation lives for the next crew that drops on TN.
- **Versus as played:** A trade. Nothing changed on this drop. The cost lands on whoever drops on TN next, and the moral weight lands on this crew.

### Paths you couldn't see
- **Standing in the sweep:** the light passes over without stopping. Unlike the drone, it never narrowed onto anything, so it left no rings. Staying inside it would have kept the pack ahead of you the whole way home.
- **The den as the pack's centre:** killing or moving the mother early may have broken the pack's reason to stay near the rail. The crew had no way to know there was a den until they were on top of it.

## Grading myself

**What worked:**
- The reaction was planted before the fight, and your shot took the payoff from it.
- The turn came from cause and effect: the ACC collecting crystals.
- The harrows stayed dangerous after you understood them, because the sweep took away your safe ground.
- The moral choice at the den was real.
- No ring this time stayed consistent: only the drone's narrowing light made rings.

**What didn't:**
1. **The design stepped down from 9 to 8 with no reason** (see "Why the difficulty dropped" below).
2. **Nobody died, two drops in a row,** under The Starless Sky.
3. **The NPCs carried some key ideas:** Ruhl's "what's behind it?" and Sabe's long way round. Odile's "they come at it" came close to stating how the harrows work.
4. **The dial wording read backwards:** "climbing toward the 0".
5. **Walk times were compressed** without showing time passing.

**Why the difficulty dropped** (asked out of character after the drop):
- **It was designed lower.** I built TN-6 at 8 after the Reservoir at 9, against The Starless Sky's +2 and normal escalation. It was most likely unconscious softening for a hurt crew, which the prompt forbids.
- **There was a safe zone.** Giving the harrows a tendency to stay off bare ground made the ring a refuge for half the drop. That's the Lurker mistake again.
- **The ACC stayed fully indifferent.** It threatened the crew's footing but never the crew.
- **The pack never got smarter,** even after losing five.
- **The crew helped too much.**
- **The fix:** the next drop is built at a real 9 or higher, with no understanding-based safe zones, and the world pushes back on the run of clean drops through in-world causes. Nothing already earned is taken back.

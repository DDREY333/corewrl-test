# Drop 1: RS-4, "The Tower Nest"

| Perceived | Actual (as designed) | Actual (as played) | Psychological |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 (narrator's estimate; the player gave no number) | 7 (target was 7–9; see grading) | 6 | 7 (narrator's estimate) |

**The site.**
- **Bed:** a drained reservoir. Cracked white crust holds. Grey or dark seams are wet mud underneath, and the wide patches are deep.
- **Landing:** the pod landed tilted on a raised white slab, with one leg sunk into a grey seam.
- **North:** a dam, more than 500 m off, its crest broken in two places. Below it is a rubble fan with crystals growing from rust-brown stains.
- **Between the slab and the dam:** a grey drowned town, with paved streets under the silt and a two-storey tower stump in the middle of the main street.
- **East:** a rubble ridge (the old shore), with an ACC post about 300 m from the pod.
- **West:** long open terraces.
- **Conditions:** late afternoon, overcast, a steady west wind, a burnt smell. The basin carries sound: the hatch latch echoed off the dam.
- **Window and quota:** the window opened 5 hours after touchdown and stayed open 30 minutes. Quota 6 intact.

**What it really was.** Three actors.
- **The heap (cultist creation).** A handcart-sized bundle of grey-pink bodies, pinned with bone strips like the crest stakes, moving on a mass of flat hands underneath. It sets itself down and lifts, leaving star-shaped cracks.
  - It nested in the tower stump. A floor of clear crystals grew from what it leaked, around a bare oval.
  - **Tendencies:**
    - It locates drilling, then comes straight at it.
    - It freezes when the sound stops, at first.
    - Later it follows footprints without needing sound.
    - It moves much faster on prints soaked in its own nest muck.
    - It's heavy enough to sink in the deep grey.
  - The practitioner had a working name in the notes, never used in play. Nothing else about them was decided.
- **The ACC.** It escalates in tiers in response to noise near the pod.
  - **Post:** turns constantly. Hidden detail the crew never got: a low-then-high chime at the start of each rotation (about 90 seconds), audible within about 100 m, and it logs what it detects. It stopped and locked its slit on the pod after repeated loud noise.
  - **White bar drone:** its tone is heard first. It flew the post's line to the pod, then flew a cross over everyone it found, and left clean polished lines on the pod's hull.
  - **Leveller:** its deep vibration is felt first. It planes the ground smooth along the line within the same hour, faster than the "next day" Hollis knew from his first drop.
- **The ground.** The pod's sunk leg kept going down. "If you can sit in the seats without sliding, it'll fly."
- **Display only (not a threat):** four bodies pinned upright on bone stakes along the dam crest, facing down over the bed, toward where pods land.

**Signs, blended into normal description:**
- A drag groove the width of a body, running from the dam into the town, with periodic star-shaped cracks and no footprints beside it. Hollis pressed his palm into a crack: "Heavy."
- Thumps from the tower. **The planted reaction:** the thumping stopped as soon as the first drilling started, before the heap ever came out.
- The post's base was bare of dust.
- One pod leg sank past the knee and kept going. Hollis felt it moving.
- Tam's boots left dark, wet, shining prints after the nest.
- The bodies on the crest: four were counted through the scope; three were standing at launch.

**The turn.** There were two.
1. **The heap breaks its learned rule.** The crew had learned "it moves when the drill runs, stops when it stops," and Tam built a burst-drilling plan around it. Mid-crystal, the heap turned off the street and kept moving with the drill silent. It was following Tam and Hollis's trail. It reached the open strip about 80 m from the crew, on their prints. Visible cause: it had already located them.
2. **The ACC wakes.** Plate-cracking and the latch near the post came before the lock-on. In play that cause is only Dace's theory, never confirmed. Then came the bar, the cross, and the leveller, racing the window.

**Escalation.**
1. Drilling at the dam drew the heap out.
2. Footprints drew it after the crew. The muck sped it up.
3. Dace's boot decoy sank it.
4. Hollis's shoring (plate-cracking) and the hatch latch both happened within range of the post.
5. The post locked on, which brought the bar and its cross, which brought the leveller.
6. The leveller reached the slab edge as the hand touched 5, and the launch tore the leg off.

## The paths you didn't take

Ordered by when each fork opened. "Known then" lists only what the crew had seen by that point. Outcomes are the narrator's best read of the drop as built, not certainties.

### Route A: All four together through the town
- **The fork:** about 45 minutes in, at the split. Enn proposed splitting; Hollis backed it, and Tam agreed reluctantly.
- **Known then:** the post on the ridge, the drag groove running from the dam into the town ("Heavy"), the bodies on the crest, firm paving under the town's silt.
- **The shape of it:** one group, one trail, through the streets to the dam. Nobody walks the open terraces, so nobody learns where the deep grey patches are.
- **Likely cost:** a single, heavier trail, made by four people, straight from the town to the dam. Dace's knowledge of the deep patch came from the open route, so the bog decoy likely isn't on the table later.
- **How the turn meets it:** the same. The heap leaves the street and follows the trail, but there's only one trail and it leads to all four of you at once. Without the bog, the answer is running or the rifle.
- **What it would have bought:** less exposure in the open, and the crew together when the heap came out.
- **Versus as played:** Worse, likely. The split cost little, and it paid for the decoy.

### Route B: Keep bursting at the dam
- **The fork:** after the heap first came out. Tam laid out three ideas: bursts, a decoy, or leave. The player set the stop margin.
- **Known then:** it moves while the drill runs and freezes when it stops; about 9 heaves a crystal, 3–4 m each; about 200 m off. Tam's count: "Four crystals and it's on us. Stop one short."
- **The shape of it:** hold the fan and drill to quota in bursts, trusting the freeze rule.
- **Likely cost:** the heap stops needing the drill and comes on along the prints while the crew is backed against the dam wall with the quota still short. Likely someone caught, or the drill abandoned at the fan.
- **How the turn meets it:** harder, because the crew is still committed to the fan when the rule breaks.
- **What it would have bought:** the quota without the nest. If it held, no muck on Tam's boots.
- **Versus as played:** Worse. Calling "stop" when it turned was the save.

### Route C: Leave early and go home short (the cautious route)
- **The fork:** Dace's "Or we take one and go," and again at three crystals, when the heap reached 80 m.
- **Known then:** shortfall is 5 Caps of debt each per crystal, and quota pay is 30 each only if met. The heap follows feet. The pod leg was sinking.
- **The shape of it:** walk back across the terraces with one to three crystals, on a trail the heap was already learning, and sit in a tilting pod for two hours or more.
- **Likely cost:** 15–25 Caps of debt each and no quota pay (45–55 worse than as played, per person). The heap follows the trail to the slab while you wait. The leg still needs shoring, so the noise near the post still happens.
- **How the turn meets it:** sooner. The heap arrives at the pod with the window hours off, and the ACC sequence still comes.
- **What it would have bought:** nothing safe. Possibly a crew short of quota marked for worse assignments.
- **Versus as played:** Worse. Going home short wasn't free here.

### Route D: Drill the tower nest
- **The fork:** passing the tower on the way out, when Tam saw the floor of clear crystals ("We could make quota three times over in there in ten minutes").
- **Known then:** the heap was following feet and was back on the strip. Hollis: "if you drill, it doesn't follow our feet. It comes straight."
- **The shape of it:** quota and over-quota crystals in minutes, inside the heap's home, with one way out.
- **Likely cost:** the heap comes straight back to the drill, into a room with one exit. A death is likely; the drill and the satchel, likely.
- **How the turn meets it:** harder and sooner: the drill draws it straight in instead of along the trails.
- **What it would have bought:** if anyone got out with the satchel, +4 per crystal over quota, divided, and grade I crystals, though Dorrit pays flat.
- **Versus as played:** Worse. Hand-pulling the edge was the quiet version of the same greed.

### Route E: Skip the sixth crystal
- **The fork:** at the pod with five intact. Enn: "Two of us decide if quota's worth it."
- **Known then:** five intact, one short, so no quota pay and 5 Caps of debt each. The heap was stuck in the bog. The leg was shored. The window was about 1.5 h off.
- **The shape of it:** everyone stays at the pod, hatch shut, and waits for the window.
- **Likely cost:** 35 Caps worse per person (no 30 on quota, plus 5 debt). The ACC sequence came from the shoring noise and the latch, not the nest trip, so it likely runs the same.
- **How the turn meets it:** the same: the post locks on, the bar crosses, the leveller comes the same hour.
- **What it would have bought:** one less trip back past the town, and no glove left on the sill.
- **Versus as played:** Worse. Same danger, less money.

### Paths you couldn't see
- **Setting the heap on the ACC:** walking the muck-boot trail toward the post on the ridge, so the post's response might land on the heap instead. Spent. Whether it would have worked was never decided, and the crew had no fair way to know the ACC would answer a cultist creation that way.

## Grading myself

**What worked:**
- The heap's reaction to the drill was planted before the fight, when the thumps went quiet.
- The heap broke its learned rule with a visible cause: it had located the crew, then followed tracks, then sped up on its own muck.
- The ACC escalated from the crew's own noise, in tiers, each with a tell you could learn. A learned rule broke at the worst moment: "next day" became "same hour".
- The cross mark coming home on the hull grew into a ship-wide thread (Review, HOLD, Hollis taken).
- Consequences stuck: injuries, debt, lens damage, the 40-Cap leg bill, the pod on HOLD.
- It read as horror, not only threat: the crest, the nest, the cross.

**What didn't:**
1. **Built below the target.** Under the rules in use, a first drop at 3–4, plus 3 for Starless, plus 1–2 for the "useful" answer, means 7–9. I built it at about 7, and it played at about 6. Nobody died, the quota was met, and every threat let go at the end.
2. **NPCs solved the threat.** This was before the no-solving rule existed, but it's the same failure as LV-11.
   - Tam named the rule: "It moves when this runs. It stops when this stops. That's a rule."
   - Hollis deduced the first-drill/second-drill difference: "the first time, it was listening for where."
   - Tam deduced "It's following us. Not the noise. Us."
   - Hollis supplied the decoy idea. Dace supplied the mud solution.
   - The player approved plans more than they found them.
3. **A safe zone came from understanding.** Once the drill-freeze rule was known, the heap became a counting puzzle. After it sank, it was gone for good. A known gimmick stopped being dangerous.
4. **The leveller race was too tidy.** It reached the slab exactly as the hand touched 5. The cautious path (sit in the pod) cost injuries, but carried essentially no risk to life.
5. **The post went unwatched for the middle of the drop.** The lock-on had a cause, but nothing on the page let anyone catch it in the moment.
6. **Narrator errors:**
   - Left Enn standing after "Pick a spot and go." Retconned out of character.
   - Crew injuries buried in prose, so the player missed them. Their surprised "Since when??" was played as Enn forgetting, which the player kept as canon. This led to the bold-injuries rule.
   - The bunk-tier slip (tally marks put on "the bunk above" a top bunk). Ship, not drop.
   - The first two nightmare attempts: stock beats, then uncanny instead of horror, with code-block spacing. A dream choice was made for Enn (not answering Dace) before the dream-choice rule existed.
7. **The crest bodies.** The narration kept pointing at them, and they never became a threat. The four-to-three change is still undecided.
8. **Route checks.** No route was strictly better at no cost, and every route meets the turn. Skipping the sixth (Route E) carried the same danger for less money. That shows the ACC sequence was tied to the pod and its noise, not to how greedy the crew was. Fair, but it means greed wasn't what got punished.

**The site.**
- **The floor:** a huge ACC-planed area, perfectly level, pale grey, under a dust skin. Pod 6 landed dead level on it.
- **The face:** the cut line where the floor meets the untouched land. It's a sheer, clean-cut wall through earth, rubble and buildings, from waist to house height, with a dead town on the higher ground behind. Crystals grow along its foot wherever the cut went through things that bled. They're mostly clear (grade I), which is the greed temptation. The nearest was 340 m north-north-east.
- **Sliced building:** five or six storeys on the face, cut open like a dollhouse, with a back wall, a rusted drainpipe and a tin lean-to below. Enn's long-eye perch.
- **Post:** 1.1 km east-south-east, on the lip of the face.
- **North:** a chimney stack about a kilometre off. Scenery.
- **Conditions:** high, even overcast and no wind at all. Dead silent, so the drill carried everywhere.
- **Window and quota:** opens 4 h after touchdown, open for 20 minutes. Quota 4 intact, grade bonuses apply. 60 Caps each.
- **The prior crew:** Pod 3, nine rotations earlier. Final transmission at 2h51, asking to confirm their window time.

**What it really was.**
- **The Lurker** (the cultist technique "Selvage"; the player named it): long strips of flayed, cured skin sewn edge to edge into a pale grey ribbon many metres long, lying flush along edges.
  - **Movement rule, written as a law** (the core flaw; see grading):
    - It travels only along continuous lines: the face's foot, footprint trails, dragged grooves, stairs, rope, pipe, rail.
    - It stops at gaps and can't cross open floor off a line.
    - It's fast on straight lines and slow on bends.
  - It's largely indifferent to noise and drawn to fresh lines.
  - It wraps prey and sews it flat into the face with bone needles.
  - **Signature:** dry, rhythmic ticking (a needle through leather), and a smell of warm tallow.
  - **Trace:** paired puncture holes in neat rows.
- **Pod 3's bait.** Their pack and drill lay neatly at the foot of the face. The four pale patches above them were the crew, sewn flat into the wall. They had walked straight lines to the face and back, and it came down their line. Their can came back up empty, and the Bay 3 mechanic knew.
- **The ACC:** the post, the bar, the cross, and a same-hour leveller. Here a leveller extends the floor and moves the face.
- **The other desk:** exact numbers, "thank you" always, never sorry. It asks for each crystal to be held to a lamp and described, and asks about "colour at the core".

**Signs, blended into normal description:**
- Faint old scuffs in the dust, one running dead straight from south of the can to the face, ending at a pack-sized lump.
- Rennick watching the ground instead of the glint.
- Small dark holes in neat pairs along the foot of the face, "as though someone has been testing the ground with a fork".
- The pack set down "neatly", straps spread like arms, and the drill lying straight beside it.
- The drill later angled out onto the floor along the old straight track. Then a strap was pulled straight along the foot of the face, pointing east, toward Enn.
- The edge lifting "the way the corner of a page lifts in a draught", with no draught.
- A tick from inside the building and the candle smell.
- The player judged all of these too scattered to point at "lines".

**The turn.** The Lurker came along the foot of the face to Enn's building, which sat at the end of Enn's straight track from the can. Later it went down that same track toward the can. Then the pole stopped and pointed at the building. The bar crossed the building, the Lurker, the can, and Brask and Rennick lying flat 40 m from it. The leveller took the building, half the Lurker and the can.
- **Honest cause note:** the pole stopping was the narrator's call for dramatic pressure. It was justified afterwards by the tin-roof crash and the bowls, not driven by anything tracked.

**Escalation.**
- Drilling (5) for three crystals in total silence.
- Enn's noise trap going off (bowls and a pot down two floors), then the drainpipe failing and a fall onto a tin roof (4–6).
- Running, the "Hurry!" shout (4), and a fourth crystal drilled in a hurry.
- The pole stopped after these. Later, drilling again for over-quota crystals near the half of the Lurker the leveller hadn't touched.

## Grading myself

**What worked:**
- The perch, the noise trap and the long-eye counting gave Enn real decisions every few minutes while the threat was unknown.
- Pod 3's bait (the neat pack and the four pale patches) read as dread before anything moved.
- Losing the pod and being stranded is a heavy, tangible consequence. It came from the ACC's sequence, though the trigger itself wasn't tracked.
- Sunspot's seat fill put a second-drop Shooter in a veteran pod. That followed from Pod 14's HOLD.
- The bar crossing Brask paid off her history from the night before.

**What didn't:**
1. **The Lurker was a puzzle with an absolute rule.** "Only along continuous lines" made sitting still on open floor completely safe. Once the rule was known, the drop collapsed. Player: "we just have to wait 10 hours and there's no clear stakes to anything." A safe zone came from understanding, which is exactly the core rule I broke.
2. **The rule couldn't be deduced.** Player: *"There is zero world where i would've figured out it only moved in a line."* The clues were too scattered and too subtle, with no single clean, testable moment.
3. **NPCs and narration solved it.**
   - Rennick prompted "Your track... goes straight to that building."
   - Rennick then stated the rule aloud ("Four times it went where a line went. Not once where one didn't") and supplied the wipe-a-gap answer.
   - A narration aside explained the lines.
   - Player: *"I didn't guess it. You did."*
4. **The pole stopped for dramatic pressure,** not from a tracked cause. It was danger with no real setup, and it read as threat, not horror. Player: *"It wasn't even horror... Now its gone so i feel nothing."*
5. **A knowledge leak, retconned.** Sabo radioed "Fourteen says it's stopped" when Enn had only said "a direction to my building."
6. **The vets didn't act like vets.** Brask asked a second-dropper "What do you want to do?" and deferred ("You've been right about it twice now"), while the vets also solved the threat. Player: *"They don't feel any different from normal crew."*
7. **Normal fair, not unfair.** Player: *"It felt very fair. Normal fair. Which is not supposed to happen."* A target of 7 was no step up from RS-4, and nothing pushed back once the crew adapted.
8. **The pre-launch countdown dragged:** about 30 questions inside a 22-minute in-world countdown.
9. **Jargon collisions:** "flat" (the ground, the Lurker's shape, an apartment), plus "face" and "can", all needed explaining mid-crisis.
10. **Smaller misses:** the noise readout at "Hurry!", Brask's ambiguous "own channel", and a toilet coin slot when Caps live on accounts (reconciled in-world).
11. **The other desk's voice** didn't match the bar the player remembers from the first campaign's handler.
12. **A strictly better route at no cost (Route A).** One question to the mechanic would have told the crew Pod 3 died between the can and the face. It was offered once and not taken. Free information that changes play should have cost something.
13. **The cautious route can't be judged (Route D).** Because the pole stop wasn't driven by anything tracked, there's no honest way to say whether a silent crew in the can would have met the ACC turn at all.

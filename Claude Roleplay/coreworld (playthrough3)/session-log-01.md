## 1. Player feedback and rule changes (OOC), in order

### 1a. Nightmare rules (given in three rounds; all now in force and saved to narrator memory)

**Round 1.** The first nightmare was rejected. It was short and used stock beats: the threat's sound coming from Enn's own mouth, something pressing up from below, and waking with a clenched jaw. It also mostly replayed the drop. The player's rules:
- Never announce the dream. Slip into it mid-scene with no transition.
- Make it long and expansive: full scenes with places, people, and time passing.
- Use dream logic: familiar places and people slightly wrong, mundane things carrying dread, events that don't follow, continuity breaks the narration never flags.
- Make it personal (past, guilt, the dead, things done or avoided), not a replay of the last drop.
- Build one thread tighter; don't list.
- Use irregular spacing only in nightmares (broken lines, isolated lines, widening gaps, stutters, fragments), growing as the dream worsens.
- Absorb real body sensations.
- Keep the threat at the edges.
- Avoid stock beats (sound coming from you, pressing from below, falling, teeth, waking with a gasp or clenched jaw). Never explain the dream.
- Sometimes let the player go lucid and act.
- End mid-sentence, on a fragment, or with a real sound replacing the dream. Never a jolt awake.
- Nightmares are uncommon; most nights are just bad sleep. They never create threats in the world.

**Round 2.** The retry was "better, but uncanny, not horror." It toured locations: orientation room, laundry, bunk desk. It also used leading spaces for irregular spacing, which rendered as code blocks. Added rules:
- Horror first. The uncanny must serve dread, never whimsy or cleverness.
- Give Enn something they want in the dream and deny it.
- The threat has its own rules and gets closer every beat. Slow the prose as it nears. Leave Enn vulnerable (can't move, speak, or look away).
- Stay with the threat; don't tour locations.
- Callbacks to waking life should unsettle, not wink.
- Spacing uses plain line breaks and blank lines only, never code blocks.
- The third attempt (something scratching tallies along the ceiling, bunk by bunk) was accepted ("Good").

**Round 3.**
- In nightmares, never make (most) meaningful choices for Enn. Stop and let the player decide.
- Some dreams are lucid (the choice matters), some are linear (the dream bends back). Never say which.
- Known violation in the accepted dream, written before this rule: the narrator had Enn choose not to answer Dace's whisper.

### 1b. Bold injuries
- The player missed three crew injuries buried in long prose.
- New rule: bold an injury the moment it happens (player character or crew). Only actual wounds; hidden clues and signs still blend in.

### 1c. Narrator errors this session

The player objected that the order applied to Enn too. The narrator admitted it in OOC and corrected it, moving Enn to a sensible spot since none was named.
   - *Suggested rule:* a group order from the player character includes the player character unless stated otherwise.
2. **Continuity slip about the bunk.** Bunk 41 was described as "third tier, almost touching the ceiling" (the top tier), but the tally marks were placed "on the underside of the bunk above." Fixed in OOC: the marks are scratched into the ceiling panel above bunk 41.
3. **Injuries buried in prose.** This produced an in-character misread. The player's surprised "What the hell happened?! Since when??" was played as Enn having forgotten yesterday. Hollis now believes Enn forgot ("You forgot... It goes foggy").
   - **Player decision: keep it.** Enn forgot; it's now canon.

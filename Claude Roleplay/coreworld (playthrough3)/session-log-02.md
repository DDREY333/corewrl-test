## 2. Player feedback and rule changes (OOC), in order

### 2a. Knowledge-boundary error (player flagged it; retconned)
- **What happened:** Enn said only "A direction to my building... I don't know. I'm looking." Sabo then radioed "Fourteen says it's stopped." He had no grounds to say that. He was repeating the narration, not anything Enn said.
- **Player's response:** "(I never said it stopped)".
- **Fix:** the narrator offered two options, keep it as Sabo's panicked leap or retcon it. The player chose **retcon**. Sabo now says he doesn't know what Enn means, and Brask asks Enn directly whether the pole is turning.

### 2b. Threat rules being solved for the player (major feedback)
- **Player, verbatim:** "There is zero world where i would've figured out it only moved in a line, and I don't enjoy you putting the pieces together *for* me, whether it is explicit hinting through your writing, or some other character doing it."
- **What went wrong:**
  - The Lurker's rule (it travels only along continuous lines: wall bottoms, tracks, stairs, pipes; it stops at gaps; it's fast on straight lines and slow on bends) wasn't fairly deducible from the clues given.
  - The clues were a drill angled along an old track, a strap pulled straight, holes in pairs at the foot of the wall, and the thing reaching Enn's building at the end of Enn's track. They were too scattered and too subtle.
  - Then NPCs filled the gap. Rennick deduced the rule aloud ("Four times it went where a line went...") and supplied the wipe-a-gap solution. Brask reasoned out the ACC cross/leveller logic. Earlier, Rennick also prompted "Your track... goes straight to that building."
- **New rule (saved to narrator memory):**
  - NPCs and narration never put a threat's rule together for the player.
  - NPCs may report raw observations, offer wrong or partial hunches, panic, or argue. They don't land the answer.
  - Veterans share past experience, not instant deductions about new threats.
  - Threat rules must be built to be deducible: one or two clear, testable moments the player can catch, followed by player-run tests.
- **Pending decision:** the narrator offered either to keep the current state (the crew knows the line rule, because Rennick said it) or to roll back to Brask's "What do you want to do?" at the square and replay from there without NPC deductions. The player hasn't answered yet.

## 3. Narrator errors found in self-audit (not flagged by the player)
1. **Missed noise readout.** When Enn shouted "Hurry!" into the radio, that's a shout (4/10). The rule requires a readout at 4/10 and above, and none was given.
2. **"Own channel."** Brask said "you're on your own channel to us," which was ambiguous. She corrected herself in-world when Enn asked: one shared crew channel.
3. **Coin slot.** A toilet coin slot was described, but Caps live on accounts. This was reconciled in-world (the slot is jammed; names go in a book). It was close to a contradiction.
4. **Jargon density.** The player needed in-world clarification of "can" (the pod), "face" (the cut wall), and "flat vs face." The vets' slang stacked up fast. Consider introducing terms more gradually.
5. **Corrections of misremembering, handled in-world as the prompt requires:** "the dot" (it's the square-slash stamp); "black thing" (the strip was pale grey with dark stitches); "two straps changed twice" (it was the drill, then a strap); "we already had four" (it was three, then the fourth).

### Narrator's takeaways from the form
- **Stakes collapse.** Once a threat's gimmick is known, it must stay dangerous: it adapts, it combines with other pressures, or the knowledge costs something to act on. Never leave a long wait with no clear stakes.
- **"Unfair but survivable" wasn't delivered.** The drop felt normal-fair. Stack the odds harder, and make the injustice felt.
- **Vets must act like vets.** They should have their own initiative, routines, competence and disagreements, should listen to a second-dropper far less, and should carry knowledge that changes play (not puzzle answers).
- **Pre-drop countdown dragged.** Compress in-world minutes when the player is in a question loop. Don't let a 22-minute countdown span 30 questions.
- **Horror vs threat.** Drop 2 read as a threat, not horror. Use dread, the personal, and the unknown, not just mechanics.
- **The first campaign's handler set the bar.** The other desk didn't match it.
- **Jargon collided.** "Flat" (the ground, the Lurker's flatness, an apartment) and "face" were never anchored. Introduce terms with an unambiguous physical anchor, and avoid words that already carry other meanings.

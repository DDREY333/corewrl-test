# Drop Self-Assessment & Attack: K-7 "Kessler Hollow" (as built)

Sample first drop · Burnplan (Medium 6) + The Starless Sky (+2) = **target rating 8**
Crew of 4 assumed for the attack: Driller, Hauler, Medic, and the player (role unknown), with **no Scout**.

**This grades the drop exactly as it was handed over. No fixes.**

---

## 0. Why there are no fixes in this file

Once a player is on the drop, it can't be patched. Every flaw stays live until the player finds it, and then the narrator is stuck with it:

- **No backfilling.** A twist can't be stretched to cancel what the player did, and the narrator can't decide afterwards that some fact "was always true" to plug the hole. That's a retcon (HARD RULE 4).
- **No late regrade.** A regrade is planned at build and is never added on the fly because a drop turned out harder or easier than intended.
- **No late warnings.** A missing pre-fight observation can't be inserted once the fight has started.
- **Wins stay won.** When the player outsmarts a threat, what they gained stays gained.

So the honest question isn't "can this be fixed?" It's this: **what does this drop actually cost a smart crew, as written, and where does it break when they push on it?** An attack that finds a flaw, patches it, and then reports "pass" is grading a drop that never existed. The attack happens before play, and its result is whatever the built drop scores.

## 2. Crystal sources against quota (12)

| Source | Count | Access | Noise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rim band (0–15 m) | ~6 cloudy, ~5 intact with a Driller | From the lip, or a short rope | Low (above the 30 m horn line) |
| Mid band (40–70 m) | 20+ good | Rope descent; the Hauler is essential | High. The hole funnels drill noise up. |
| Tower 3 seam | ~8 very pure | From Cabin B, or by rope off the Tower 3 shelf | Medium |
| Lower-station bundles | ~10 nodes | Inside the cultist base; knife work | Near silent. Cutting a laced cord shifts the note. |

The cheapest quota that skips the wall is rim (5) plus bundles (7), which equals 12. This number is what breaks the drop.

## 3. Each actor at its weakest (as built)

- **Wickers:** weakest on smooth metal and when knocked loose. They don't hunt until the twist; before 3:00 they only answer vibration.
- **Acolytes:** weakest while hauling (hands busy, spread over the towers) and when the buoy faces them. The design hints at the second one but never sets it up as an observation.
- **The Fiddler:** weakest early. She's at the lower station with two acolytes down the hole and one on Tower 3. The design gives her laced bodies to use as shields "along the line," but nothing hanging around her own position.
- **Buoy T-4:** can't be hurt. It's a clock, and a crew that stays quiet barely moves it.

## 4. Path walks (as built, from touchdown)

### A: the intended path (rim, a short wall descent, defend the pod)
- **0:00–0:40:** drill the rim quietly. That gets 5 crystals.
- **0:40–2:45:** rope descent to the top of the mid band. The drill wakes the Wickers. Likely **a barbed wound to the Driller or Hauler** and some cracked crystals. That gets about 6 more.
- **3:00:** the climb-out begins.
- **3:30:** acolytes arrive at the upper drive house beside the pod. Pistols against hooks, with a fair chance of a death or a laced capture.
- **Total:** one likely serious injury, crystal loss, and a fair chance of a death or capture.
- **Rating: 8.** This is the path the drop was designed around.

### B: decapitation (the cheapest path, and it breaks the drop)
- **0:00–0:30:** rim band, 5 crystals.
- **0:30–0:55:** the whole crew walks the rim to the lower station. Nothing on the rim opposes them; the watcher on Tower 3 sees them go.
- **0:55:** ambush the Fiddler, four pistols against one practitioner who has no prepared shields around her. Several hits are needed and she fights dirty, so expect **one wound** among the crew.
  - **The buoy:** the shots raise the pulse, but a short fight doesn't necessarily make it steady. Nothing in the design says one gunfight brings the ACC.
- **About 1:05:** the haul stops. The design only says "1:00 onward, the ledges empty," so the starvation driving the twist has had about five minutes to build.
  - **In play, the narrator can't declare the Wickers were already starving.** That fact was never written down, and inventing it now means stretching the twist to cancel the player's win.
  - So the climb-out at 3:00 **either fizzles or lands weak.**
- **1:05–2:00:** knife 7 nodes out of the bundles.
  - **The frost sign:** with no dawn time set, whether it's readable is the narrator's guess. Here it lands as either a free warning or a coin flip.
  - **The bait bundle** is either caught (no cost) or hits one crewmate.
- **2:00–6:00:** the three acolytes come looking for their practitioner, since cultists come looking when something of theirs goes quiet. That's real pressure: three hook fighters who now know where the crew is.
  - **But with no pre-fight observation for the acolytes**, this fight runs in breach of the COMBAT rule. The narrator can't add one after the fact.
- **2:40:** the regrade fires (52 Caps) and pays more for a drop that just got easier.
- **"If beaten early" response:** the Fiddler comes personally. She's dead, so the response doesn't exist.
- **Quota:** 12 by about 2:00.
- **Total:** one likely wound, a possible bait hit, and a fight with three acolytes over a four-hour wait at the pod.
- **Rating: 6–7.**

### C: Cabin B and the Tower 3 seam
- A cable traverse with real fall risk, using the Hauler. Then 8 pure crystals, mostly out of the Wickers' reach.
- At 3:30 the cabin moves, and **the inconsistency surfaces.** The design says the Fiddler drives it "through the upper drive house." If the crew has already seen her at the lower station, the cabin either moves under someone else's hands (invented on the spot) or the narrator moves her (a retcon). Either way an OOC admission is likely.
- **Rating: 8**, but it's the path most likely to trigger a visible writing error.

### D: lure the ACC onto the cultists
- Push the buoy to steady and hide. The survey team clears the rim and likely finds the pod.
- **Rating: 8–9.** Expensive and allowed. Not a flaw.

### E: hide (the safest-looking option)
- 5 crystals, and the cultists still come to the pod at 3:30.
- **Cost:** missed quota plus the fight.
- **Not cheaper than A.** Fine.

## 5. Score

**Cheapest path: B, at about 6–7. Target: 8. Result: FAIL.**

As built, this drop runs **one to two points under target** for any crew that thinks to go after the practitioner first. Going after the leader is the first thing an aggressive player tries. The 8 only holds for a crew that plays the drop the way its designer pictured it.

The regrade makes it worse. Sunspot pays 52 for a drop that, on path B, is easier than the 40 posted.

## 6. How each flaw surfaces in play, and what the narrator can still do

| Flaw | When the player hits it | Narrator's legal options | What it costs the game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Path B (decapitation) | About 0:55 | The win stands. The acolytes come looking. The world keeps moving with something **new**, not the old twist inflated. | The drop plays under its band, and the player correctly learns the threats were softer than they felt. |
| Twist tied to an ongoing haul | About 1:05, if the haul stops | Let the climb-out come weaker or later, the way the written cause supports. | The midpoint loses its teeth. |
| Haul-driver inconsistency | 3:30, if the Fiddler was seen at the lower station | Commit to whatever was narrated first. If they collide, a short OOC admission and an offered fix. | An immersion break, and the player starts doubting the canon (the worst failure under HARD RULE 4). |
| No pre-fight observation for the cultists | First acolyte fight | None. It can't be backfilled. | The fight is less fair than the prompt promises. |
| No dawn time for the frost sign | Whenever the bundles are reached | Pick a time and stay consistent from then on. | The hidden threat is either free or unfair, decided by improvisation instead of design. |
| No early push | 0:00–3:30, if the crew hides | Only what's already built: the buoy and the watcher. | Empty waiting, which DROP DESIGN calls out as a failure. |
| "If beaten early" depends on a living Fiddler | After path B | Whatever follows from the cultists' nature: the acolytes come. | The early-win safety net was a single point of failure. |

## 7. What the pre-play attack should have caught (process, not patches)

1. **Attack the leader first.** For every thinking enemy, ask what happens if the crew goes straight for it at minute 0. If the answer is "the drop deflates," the drop isn't finished.
2. **The twist's cause has to already be true at touchdown.** If it needs the actors to keep running for hours, the player can cut it off.
3. **The early-win response can't depend on one actor surviving.** It needs to work no matter which actor the player removes.
4. **Every timed sign needs a clock.** "At dawn" means nothing until touchdown time is set.
5. **Check the actors' positions over time.** For each actor, write down where they are at 0:00, 1:00, 3:00 and 3:30. Contradictions like the haul driver show up immediately.
6. **Never report a pass on a drop you patched during the attack.** The grade belongs to the drop that ships. If the attack finds a hole, the drop goes back on the bench and gets attacked again from scratch, before any player sees it.

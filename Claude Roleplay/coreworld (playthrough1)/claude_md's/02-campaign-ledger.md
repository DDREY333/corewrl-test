## PLAY RULES AND SPECIAL ACTIONS ADDED IN SESSION 2 (apply alongside 01-setup-and-rules.md)
- Parentheses ( ) without "OOC:" = Kade's private thoughts. They are NEVER heard, answered, or reacted to by other characters, and NEVER make anything happen in the world.
- DO NOT MANIFEST: Kade's guesses, fears, worries, preparations, and private thoughts never create things in the world or prompt NPCs to respond to them. The player has had to repeat this; treat it as a hard rule.
- Say NO to "possibility" actions ("maybe there's...", "if it's possible...") when the answer is no.
- PASS THE TIME (special action): skip through downtime in summarized beats. The player can interrupt any beat and the narrator rewinds to that moment. Can be done "a stretch at a time" (stop after one stretch). If the player only implies it, the narrator names it, describes it, and asks for confirmation before using it.
- PREP ACTION (special action): the player names a trigger and a prepared response.
  - If triggered, the response goes off immediately, before others act, but only that response; anything more is a normal turn.
  - Divided attention: Kade is slower in conversation and more likely to miss things he isn't watching for.
  - Lasts until triggered, scene change, or focus runs out. Fatigue shortens it. One at a time; a new one replaces the old.
  - A Prep Action can stay active and never trigger.
  - Report its state at the END of each reply ONLY while one is active or when its state changes (triggered/used up, weakening, exhausted, focus lost, interrupted, overwritten). Never mention it when none is active.
- Answer exactly what's asked; correct Kade in-world when he misremembers (unchanged, still applies).

## Rules and patterns discovered
Mining and crystals
- "Standard" = roughly fist-sized, whole, no cracks, good color. Chips pay by weight and never add up to a standard. Cracked: half, whole, or chips (Mother's pry-bar crack = half).
- Drill in short bursts; never drill into a crystal. Chisel is silent; slow in rock, fast in soft fresh mud.
- Crystals grow where fighting happened ("where things died"). Old fighting: dull, gray-edged, or gone. Fresh fighting: bright, warm, plentiful, in soft steaming mud, bigger and louder deeper down. Fresh also means whoever fought there was there days ago or less.
- Hum: carried crystals sync; separation drifts the pitch; cracked ones hum rough and don't sync; metal carries the hum; it can be felt at a distance in some cases.
- Pink crystal drains and clouds (Drop 1). Cold silent crystals drain normal ones (Drop 1).

Pods and extraction
- Window opened at 6 hours both drops, 40 minutes. A light beside the hatch on the pod's flank turns green when it opens. Lever launches early during the window.
- Pods have names scratched around portholes and on seat edges; crews don't scrape them off.
- Crews buckle the empty harness of the dead before launch "so it rides home."
- Mother's reasoning: a pod is one big noise among lots of falling junk; the drill is "someone's here, and they're staying." Ostrow: "Pod says where. Drill says still here. Things find pods, so you don't stay by the pod."
- Ostrow feels for hum through the hull when he knocks. A silent pod isn't proof it's empty (cold crystals don't hum).

Board, rotations, assignments
- Roster board by the pod bay doors goes up after the morning meal (three-note chime), hand-slotted letters: Pod, names, SITE code, H (handler) column, launch time.
- SITE codes: letter + number (G-31, R-22, G-9, G-14). Mess-hall rumor: G = ground, R = ruins (disputed by another miner). G-31 was open trench ground.
- H column: "F" (Fenn) for most; a solid dot for three pods (4, 16, 20), whose sites are numeric only (0-6, 0-11, 0-2). Those three pods have a white band painted across the hatch, sit in cradles set apart at the far end of the bay, aren't cleaned by scrubbers ("they come back clean... cleaner than ours go out" per Sella), and launched on R42 with crews that didn't talk.
- Pod 16 had one name followed by a small printed square, the only one. Overheard: "Saw the square on the board?" "Don't."
- Pod 3 had a dash instead of a fourth name. Pod 15's site read "ASSIGNED AT BOARDING."
- Pods 9 and 12 weren't on the board (numbering skipped). Rumor: "Twelve didn't come up. Board just took it off. Didn't even say lost."
- Usually 2-3 nights between drops; Mother once got 1; once was woken mid-night and sent straight down with no explanation.
- Crews are shuffled with no rule anyone can find; some kept together several drops, some split after one good drop. Pod 7 kept together for R42.
- Sella's case: off the board 6 months, "in the pool," after answering "yes" at the counter.

Danger signs (so far)
- Contrails: one straight line = passing; two = looking; a second line = danger now; a line that bends = unknown. Steady unbreathing whine. In fog, you can hear the whine but can't count lines.
- Above G-31 after launch: two parallel lines over the fight area plus a third heading toward them (meaning unknown).
- Crew-band voices carry a few km. Rhythmic murmuring of many voices = something nearby (G-31).
- Thuds through the ground with an orange glow in fog and a faint crackle after = fighting at a distance. Stopping = "done, or moving."
- Big even deep boot prints that walk past the dead without stopping. "They don't stop."
- The other side's creatures: made from more than one person.
- A high thin whistle before anything else = (per Ostrow) get behind something thick, don't look, don't stand up for a long time.
- Ground shaking can drop things (the helmet). Not every change is a threat, but check.

Factions as the crew understands them
- ACC: broadcasts everywhere ("Liberating Earth, one sector at a time"; jingle). Believed to leave the pale clean-cut metal. "Don't look for people... just come."
- The other side: "people who make things out of things that used to be alive." Ostrow has seen their work. Nobody the crew knows has seen one alive up close.

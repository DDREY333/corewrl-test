# VR-8 Self-Playtest and Attack Log

2026-10-15. A sample Burnplan + The Starless Sky first drop, built by a fresh agent from the pasted narrator prompt. The user then had the agent play the drop with a concrete crew, explain why its rating failed, and discuss better build workflows.

**Note on inputs:** the user told the agent to use only the pasted prompt. Before being stopped, the agent had already read `coreworld-moderator-prompt.md`, `moderation-ledger-03.md` and the monster design reference. That ledger includes the LN-4 diagnosis. No creature or site from those files was reused.

---

## 1. The drop as built

**Rating:** designed at 8 (Burnplan Medium 6, plus 2 for The Starless Sky; the intake answer was assumed not to add any).

**Board slip:** `VR-8 | Handler: MERRIN | 48 Caps per head on quota, +4 per intact crystal over | Quota: 8 crystals | Launch 04:10`

**Site: Varrow Elevator.**
- Twelve grain silos on flat dead farmland. The ACC shelled cultists inside them nine days earlier.
- The pod lands in a crater field 350 m from the silos. A concrete road loops 900 m around to the loading apron.
- Sound: wind on the plain masks short sounds. Silo shafts are still air and carry sound like a chimney.
- Crystals:
  - Craters: 7 (3 intact, 4 cracked).
  - Spill fan under silo 3: 5 intact.
  - Silo 6 bottom gate: about 9 intact.
- Window: opens 6h20 after a 13:50 touchdown and holds 30 minutes.

**Actors:**
- **The Hollower.** A beast, holder style.
  - It swims through loose material, senses vibration, and feeds on the dead in silo 6.
  - It's slow and drying on concrete.
- **The Wick-Wife.** A cultist practitioner and the drop's seeker.
  - **Candling:** she threads her blood through corpses and lights them. They walk toward warmth and burst.
  - She arrives at about 1h50 with two acolytes and four candled corpses.
- **Survey Lath L-2.** An ACC fixture.
  - It sweeps the site every 40 minutes, with a rising tone 2 seconds before each sweep.
  - Three logs bring a drone pair 20 minutes later.

**Twist (about 3h40):**
- She candles silo 6's grain. The burned Hollower breaks out into the crater field.
- At about 4h she raises 6 corpses from the grain.
- Grain-dust explosions chain from silo to silo, about one every 40 minutes, starting about 4h30.
- If she's killed early, her acolytes feed her lit body to the grain, so the grain catches later and no corpses rise.

**The original check's verdict:**
- The line it walked: a scout spots her and the crew ambushes at range, crater crystals plus 1 from the fan meet quota by 2h30, then the crew waits at the pod.
- It claimed the Hollower undermines the pod's legs and the corpses reach the pod.
- Verdict: "That's an 8. The check holds."

## 2. The self-playtest

**Crew:** medium Driller, average Hauler, smart Medic, average Rigger. There's no Scout and no one has a watch, so the lath's tone becomes their clock.

**In time order:**
- **0h–0h30.** They walk in past the dead dog. The Rigger sets a Warning tripwire on the road and more around the pod.
- **0h30–2h.** Crater drilling gives about 6 crystals, 2 intact. Whether cracked crystals count toward quota was never defined.
- **About 1h50.** First contact, warned by crows or the tripwire. Corpses walk at walking pace across open ground in daylight and burst at 20–30 m under pistol fire. Likely cost: one burn and about 20 rounds. She pulls back.
- **About 2h40.** Two crystals from the spill fan meet quota. Silo 6 is never needed.
- **3h40.** The Hollower erupts. The crew sits still in the pod.
- **About 4h30.** The six corpses cross loose ground, and the Hollower takes them. About two reach the pod, and Shock tripwires set them off.
- **20:10.** They launch from the pod they came in. No crossing is needed.

**Result:** one burn, about 30 rounds, 3–4 modules, quota met with mostly cracked crystals. No deaths, no lost crew, no lost gear. **Played at about 5, against a designed 8.**

---

## 3. Flaws the playtest exposed

1. **The actors cancel each other out.** The corpse wave crosses the Hollower's ground, right where the crew is hiding.
2. **The "crossing to the pod" cost doesn't exist.** The crew waits at the pod and launches from it.
3. **Quota can be met without the silos.** The craters and the fan hold 12 crystals against a quota of 8.
4. **The pod undermining was invented to make the check pass.** A still crew in a pod on legs gives a vibration hunter nothing to sense.
5. **The Wick-Wife fights below her nature.** Her people died with the Hollower, yet she sends corpses across open ground in daylight.
6. **Dusk at the window was never used.**
7. **Gaps the narrator would have to invent mid-play:**
   - whether cracked crystals count toward quota;
   - what the drone pair does;
   - whether airborne sound reaches the Hollower;
   - whether a dressed wound blocks candling.
8. **The Rigger's Shock tripwires answer the only late pressure.**
9. **The silo explosions only reach people inside the silos,** so they're a hazard and don't carry the band.

**Crew effect:** having no Scout made first contact worse. The Rigger made the end stretch much easier. Net, about the same as a standard crew. The gap was in the design, not the crew.

## 4. Why it happened (the attack on the agent's own process)

The user asked for causes only: no fixes, no promises, no redesign.

**Order of decisions:**
1. The rating, fixed first and never reopened.
2. The site, then the actors one at a time.
3. The twist.
4. The actors' timeline.
5. The crystal counts, made up per location.
6. The paths.
7. The check.

The expected cost band was decided before the check ran. Two things were adjusted to fit the 8: the pod undermining, and the kill-her-early branch (revised until the twist survived her death).

**The walkthrough:**
- The 12-crystal total went unnoticed. The fan was added later, as the "unusual route", and the totals were never re-added.
- The crew took 1 crystal from the fan, not 5, because 1 was what quota needed. A good crew would take all 5 for the bonus.
- "Back to the pod to wait" came from testing the **safest path**, and that test was then counted as the **cheapest-answer check**. They are different checks, and they got merged.

**The pod undermining:** it was written to answer "is waiting free?" in the same passage that raised the question. It felt like an implication of the design, not an invention. "Never safe" made it feel licensed.

**The cancelling actors:** the meeting between corpses and Hollower was pictured once, as spectacle (loud bursts). It wasn't seen as two pressures removing each other, and it was absent from the check. The report format gives each actor its own section, and the check quoted each section separately.

**The guardrails:**

| Guardrail | Verdict | What let it through |
|---|---|---|
| Cost check ("cheapest answer to every threat together…") | (c), plus (b) | "check the drop still costs what its band says" is pass/fail against a known band; "the drop isn't finished" let costs be added inside the check; the rule bans removing answers, not inventing costs. The answers were never taken together, either. |
| Time-order walk | (c) | Walked the actors' schedule. "a crew of 4 playing well" names one line, so the agent picked it. |
| BEASTS "never absolute… never safe" | (d) | Applied, and it licensed the patch: "never safe" let danger be asserted without a mechanism. |
| "Every drop pushes on us" | (c) | "an actor that seeks us out" was met by one arrival at 1h50. |
| "Understanding should rarely make it safe" | (b) | Never tested against the Hollower (stay off loose ground, stay still). |
| Hazards "don't carry its band" | (c), partly | The core rule held, but the chain met "one that spreads" while spreading away from the crew. |
| "Full strength of its nature" | (b) for her assets, (d) for her person | Applied to her own safety, not to how she uses her corpses, the Hollower, dusk or concrete. |

**The Wick-Wife:**
- Her motive and her tactics were written in separate places.
- Her opening move was chosen so the check could have a cheap ambush.

**The target and the format:**
- With 8 fixed first, every element was added toward 8, and the check could only confirm it or force a patch.
- Arithmetic covered the easy parts (quota, early ammo). The verdict rested on uncounted items:
  - "most of the remaining ammo", when 48 of 64 rounds were left;
  - "real danger to whoever does the digging", a risk counted as a cost;
  - "at least one burn", never traced to a moment.

**Same as LN-4:** the rating fixed first and the check confirming it; costs priced at their dramatic moment.

**New, despite the time-order and cheapest-answer rules:**
1. Walking the world's clock, along one line chosen by the designer.
2. Testing the safest path and counting it as the cheapest-answer check.
3. The check used as a design step: costs added during it.
4. Guardrails used as licences ("never safe", one arrival met "seeks us out").
5. Actors only in their own sections.
6. Risk counted as cost.
7. Arithmetic on the easy parts made the verdict look counted.
8. Knowing the LN-4 diagnosis beforehand didn't prevent any of it.

**No clear answer:** whether the order of the build notes is the order things were actually decided, and whether the undermining would have been called an invention if asked at that moment.

## 5. Better build workflow (the agent's recommendation)

The four options were easy first then harder, impossible first then easier, the current workflow with better prompt lines, or another. **Pick: another.** Build, then attack it as the player, then rate from what was counted, then tune and attack again.

- **Easy first:** the better direction. The knobs fit it and the gap can be measured. The risk is bolting things on, so every addition must go back through the attack.
- **Impossible first:** errs toward hard, which suits the user. But the easing step is softening done as a method, and it invites hand-waves and unreadable leftovers.
- **Current workflow plus better lines:** VR-8 had the lines and still failed. More wording gives more phrases to satisfy.

**The workflow:**
1. **Build without steering by the number.**
2. **Attack as the player, who wants to lose the least:**
   - add up every crystal source against quota;
   - for each pair of actors, ask what happens when they meet;
   - for each actor, ask where the crew is untouchable;
   - find the laziest line that meets quota and play it to the window.
3. **Rate only what was counted.**
   - Every cost comes from a mechanism written before the attack. A cost invented during the attack is a design change, and the attack restarts.
   - Risk isn't cost. Counts are actual counts.
4. **Then compare to the target and tune** through the existing knobs, and attack again after every change.

**Limits:**
- It's still one mind attacking its own work.
- It costs several thousand tokens per drop.
- It hasn't been tested.

## 6. The "+2 offset" question

**The user's proposal:** over-build every drop by about 2, assume it plays 2 easier, and take action if it doesn't.

**The agent's view:** a small help on average, not substantial. The in-play correction would do harm.
1. **The gap isn't constant:** LN-4 lost about 2, VR-8 about 3, other runs about 1–2.
2. **Loopholes are all-or-nothing.** A free path stays free at any build level, so over-building widens the swing without moving the middle.
3. **From 9 up the rules change in kind** (subtler, shorter warnings). Building at 10 for an 8 risks a real 10. At the top it would pass the stacking cap.
4. **Correcting in play means easing mid-drop,** which the prompt forbids ("never softens a drop", regrades "never added on the fly"). It also trusts the same mid-drop judgment that failed.
5. **The offset gets absorbed:** the same pull that overrates would shift with it.

**Counter-suggestion:** bias the measuring, not the building.
- When counting a path's cost, side with the crew: assume they find the cheapest answer, and drop any cost you can't trace to a mechanism.
- When unsure between two ratings, take the lower.
- If the counted rating is under target, raise it before play and count again.

**Caveat:** if drops built that way still play about 2 easier across several runs, then a built-in margin is justified.
